Christmas Apps for Mac in 2026: Lights, Snow, Santa and Trees Compared

29 Christmas and snow apps for Mac compared: where the lights hang, whether snow falls over your windows, which add a tree or Santa, and which are free.

Get on Mac App Store

Christmas apps for Mac each decorate a different part of the screen: lights along the menu bar or around the screen edge, snow falling over the desktop, a tree that stands on screen, or festive wallpapers. Most do one or two of these. Wonderfall puts snow that piles up, menu bar lights, a Christmas tree and Santa flying across the screen in one app for a one-time US$3.99.

December brings a fresh crop of these apps. Ten new ones were first released in the Mac App Store in October and November 2026 alone (Warm Mittens, Tinsel And Snow, Snow Now, Christmas: Deck the Desktop, Snowly, Festival Lights, SnowWall, Weather Desktop: Rain & Snow, Snowlit and A Coolbeans Christmas, by their App Store release dates), next to long-running apps such as Let It Snow, iSnow and Xmas snow. They hang lights in different places, treat your open windows differently and range from free to paid. This guide compares 28 of them with Wonderfall using their App Store listings and developer websites. We first checked them on 16 November 2026 and rechecked every row on 21 November, when we added Snowy!, Weatherling, Fallette, iSnow, Let It Snow and Let It Snow Desktop, and again on 25 November, when we added Flurries, SnowWall, Snow Focus, Weather Desktop: Rain & Snow, Virtual Snow, Snowlit, A Coolbeans Christmas and Festive Lights & Desktop Pets. At the end, it shows how to set up a full Christmas desktop with Wonderfall.

What kinds of Christmas apps are there for Mac?

Christmas apps for Mac fall into five groups, and many combine two or three of them.

  • Lights along the menu bar or Dock. A string of bulbs across the top of the screen, sometimes along the Dock as well. Wonderfall hangs them along the menu bar; Festivitas, Snow Now and Festive Lights & Desktop Pets can also light up the Dock.
  • Lights around the screen or your windows. MacLampsX, Desktop Lights, A Coolbeans Christmas and, since its 14 November 2026 update, Snowy! can run bulbs around the screen, and Christmas: Deck the Desktop strings them around windows.
  • Falling snow. Flakes over your desktop and, in newer apps, snow that settles on the tops of windows and piles up at the bottom of the screen. Some, such as Weatherling, Fallette, SnowWall and Weather Desktop: Rain & Snow, also bring rain, leaves or petals.
  • A tree on the desktop. A decorated tree that stands on screen, as in JoliTree, Xmas snow and Wonderfall.
  • Wallpapers, screensavers and countdowns. Extras such as the holiday wallpapers in Snowy!, the countdown in My Christmas Tree or the focus timer in Snow Focus.

Is there an app that puts Christmas lights on my Mac desktop?

Yes. Several Mac apps hang animated Christmas lights on your screen, and the main difference is where they hang: along the menu bar (Wonderfall, Festivitas, Snow Now, Festival Lights, Snowy!, Snowlit, Festive Lights & Desktop Pets), along the screen edges (MacLampsX, Desktop Lights, A Coolbeans Christmas and Snowy!’s new Around screen option) or around your windows (Christmas: Deck the Desktop, and Let It Snow’s Pro upgrade on the active window).

In Wonderfall the string hangs along the menu bar, separate from the snow, so you can use the lights, the snow or both. Eight scenes set the look in one click, among them Holiday Lights, Winter Night, Cozy Evening and New Year. After that you can change every part: 12 color palettes, 20 bulb shapes including stars, hearts, gifts and pine trees, 10 animations such as Twinkle, Chase and Candlelight, plus bulb size, spacing, brightness and glow. With Hide lights on hover, bulbs fade when your pointer passes over them, and with Reduce Motion turned on in macOS they stay steady. The Christmas lights guide covers the settings step by step.

One honest limit: Wonderfall’s lights hang only along the menu bar. They don’t run around the edge of the screen, along the Dock or around your windows. Switching from Festivitas? See how Wonderfall compares with it.

Which Christmas apps let snow fall over your windows?

Snowly, Snow Now, Christmas: Deck the Desktop, Warm Mittens, Weatherling, Fallette, iSnow, SnowWall, Snow Focus, Weather Desktop: Rain & Snow, Snowlit and Wonderfall all describe snow that lands on the tops of your windows, and Snowy! added it as an option on 14 November 2026. Snow apps also differ in whether the snow falls behind your windows or in front of them: Virtual Snow keeps it behind them, while Flurries and SnowWall let you choose.

In Wonderfall you choose the display layer. Over windows keeps the snow visible while you work, and Desktop only keeps it behind your open windows, in front of your own wallpaper. With Snow accumulation on, snow builds a drift along the bottom of the screen and, when it shows over windows, gathers on the top edges of open windows, moving with them; drag a window quickly and the snow slides off. The overlay ignores clicks, so everything underneath keeps working. Here is how an overlay differs from a snow wallpaper or screensaver.

Is there a Mac app with Santa flying across the screen?

Yes. Wonderfall sends Santa and his reindeer across your screen every so often, and so does iSnow, by its App Store description. Christmas Twinkle lists Santa’s Sleigh among its sky effects and says its Santa also flies by on Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. In the other listings checked for this guide, Santa appears only as a decoration: an ornament, a bulb design, a hat, a Christmas train in Snow Now Pro, or, in Warm Mittens Pro, a snowmobile driver clearing the drift.

In Wonderfall, open Settings, go to Holiday and set Santa flyover to Sometimes (roughly every 10 to 25 minutes) or Often (roughly every 3 to 8 minutes). Santa sound (on by default) plays his sleigh, and Call Santa now, also in the menu bar menu, brings him over straight away. He skips his flight when Reduce Motion is on or a full-screen app covers the display.

The same section has the Christmas tree: a small decorated pixel-art tree on your desktop, in Small, Medium or Large. Drag it sideways along the bottom of the screen; it keeps clear of the Dock, and its lights follow the palette and animation you picked for the menu bar lights. Since version 1.5 you can also decorate it: pick a tree style, a topper, ornaments and their colors (what’s new in Wonderfall 1.5).

Is there a free Christmas app for Mac?

Yes, several. Snowy! is free, hangs lights and can let snow pile up, Festival Lights: Holiday Glow is free and hangs lights below the menu bar, Let It Snow Desktop and Weather Desktop: Rain & Snow are free snow apps, A Coolbeans Christmas is free and runs lights around the screen edges, and MacLampsX is a beta you can download for free that does the same. Snow Focus is free, with an optional tip. Snow Now, SnowWall, Desktop Lights, Festive Lights – Mood Lights, Festive Lights & Desktop Pets and Warm Mittens are free to download and sell upgrades or light collections inside the app, and Fallette is free to try but needs a paid license to show on your desktop.

Wonderfall is a paid app: US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store, with no subscription and no in-app purchases (prices in other regions may differ). That one purchase covers the snow, lights, tree and Santa, and the rain, autumn leaves, spring petals and summer fireflies you can switch to for the rest of the year.

Why choose Wonderfall for a Christmas desktop?

Choose Wonderfall if you want the whole winter scene in one app: falling snow, a drift that grows while you work, snow on your windows, menu bar lights, a tree and Santa, all from one icon in the menu bar.

  • Snow you can play with. Pick up the shovel to dig out the drift, or choose Play in the snow to roll snowballs, stack a snowman that gets a beanie and a scarf, and throw snowballs that splat on your screen and window edges. Cleanup time! sends a little snowplow, or another vehicle you pick, across the bottom of the screen. The snowman guide shows how.
  • Your own wallpaper stays. Everything is drawn on transparent layers, and clicks pass through to your apps and desktop icons.
  • It keeps going after Christmas. Switch to rain, autumn leaves, spring petals or summer fireflies, or turn on Match local weather to let the weather where you are pick the effect. Light scenes cover New Year, Halloween and spring too.
  • Apple silicon or Intel. Wonderfall runs on both, with macOS 14.6 or later.

Where it stops: Wonderfall doesn’t hang lights around the screen edge, on the Dock or around windows, and it has no countdown, advent calendar, wallpapers or screensaver. It is an overlay that decorates your desktop while you work.

How do you set up a Christmas desktop with Wonderfall?

Install Wonderfall, then turn on snow, lights, snow accumulation, the tree and Santa in its Settings window, which opens from the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later.
  2. Turn on snow. In Weather, choose Snow and start with the Gentle or Balanced preset.
  3. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick the Holiday Lights scene.
  4. Let the snow pile up. In Surface and tools, turn on Snow accumulation. Snow builds a drift along the bottom of the screen and, when the weather shows over your windows, settles on top of them too.
  5. Add the tree and Santa. In Holiday, keep Christmas tree on, choose its size and set Santa flyover to Sometimes or Often. Press Call Santa now to see him right away.
