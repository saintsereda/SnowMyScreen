Christmas Wallpaper for Mac Is Not Enough: Make Your Own Animated Christmas Desktop

Keep any Christmas wallpaper on your Mac or MacBook and add a moving scene you tune yourself: snow, menu bar lights, a decorated tree, Santa and snow drifts.

Get on Mac App Store

A Christmas wallpaper gives your Mac or MacBook one festive picture. To make the whole desktop feel like Christmas, keep any wallpaper you like and add a moving layer of decorations on top with Wonderfall: snow you tune yourself, lights along the menu bar, a decorated Christmas tree and Santa flying past. Apple’s own wallpaper guide lists no Christmas wallpaper.

A wallpaper stays the same every day, like any fixed Christmas desktop theme. A layer you build yourself is yours alone: you decide how heavy the snow is, which lights hang along the top and how the tree is decorated, and it keeps moving while you work. This guide covers the picture underneath, each decoration on top, three ready-made looks and a New Year version.

Which wallpaper should you use as the base?

Any picture you like works as the background, whether it is a Christmas photo, a snowy landscape or the wallpaper you already have; Wonderfall draws its decorations on top and never replaces it.

Wonderfall doesn’t include or download wallpapers, so the picture comes from macOS, your own photos or a download you trust. To set it on macOS 27, follow Apple’s guide to changing your wallpaper (checked November 20, 2026):

  1. Open the settings. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, then click Wallpaper in the sidebar.
  2. Add your picture. In Your Photos, click Add Photo, then Choose File for one image or Choose Folder for a folder of them.
  3. Or use Finder. Control-click an image file and choose Set Desktop Picture.

A picture at least as large as your screen stays sharp on a MacBook Pro or an external 4K display. A calm, darker picture, such as an evening sky or a single tree, makes white snow and glowing bulbs stand out; on a very bright winter photo, choose a larger flake size or a warmer light palette.

Is there an animated Christmas wallpaper for Mac?

Not built in: Apple’s wallpaper guide lists no Christmas or holiday wallpaper, and its dynamic wallpapers brighten and darken with the time of day, so the moving Christmas part has to come from a layer on top.

Apple’s aerial wallpapers, according to the same guide, animate as you lock or unlock your Mac. Wonderfall instead draws snow, lights, a tree and Santa on transparent layers above your wallpaper. Set a layer to Desktop only, and it stays behind your windows like part of the background; set it to Over windows, and you see it while you work. Your clicks pass through either way.

How do you build your own Christmas desktop in a minute?

Install Wonderfall, then turn on snow, lights, the tree and Santa in its Settings; each takes one click, and the sections below show how to make each one your own.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later.
  2. Open Settings. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Settings…
  3. Add snow. In Weather, choose Snow and a preset: Gentle, Balanced or Intense.
  4. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick a scene, such as Holiday Lights or Winter Night.
  5. Add the tree and Santa. In Holiday, make sure Christmas tree is on and set Santa flyover to Sometimes or Often.
  6. Choose where it shows. In Weather and in Lights, set Display layer to Over windows or Desktop only.

How do you make the snow your own?

Pick the flake shapes, how heavily the snow falls and where it falls: Wonderfall has ten built-in flake shapes, three presets and a Custom option with sliders.

Use one flake shape or mix several. Start with Gentle, Balanced or Intense, then choose Custom to set Intensity, Flake size, Speed and Wind yourself. Weather area decides how much of the screen it covers: Top strip keeps the snow near the menu bar, and Full screen fills the whole display. The snow guide explains every control.

Which Christmas lights suit your desktop?

Start from a scene and change what you like: Wonderfall’s lights hang along the menu bar, and each of its eight scenes sets the palette, bulb shape and animation in one click.

The Christmas-friendly scenes are Holiday Lights, Winter Night, Cozy Evening, Celebration and New Year. After that, choose from 12 palettes, 20 bulb shapes (gifts, pine trees and stars among them) and 10 animations. Since version 1.5, Use custom colors lets you pick one to six colors of your own, and Sway with weather makes the string swing in the wind of the falling snow. The string runs along the menu bar only, not around the screen edge or on the Dock, and it has its own display layer, so it can stay on top while the snow falls behind your windows. The Christmas lights guide covers every option.

How do you put a decorated Christmas tree on your desktop?

Turn on Christmas tree in Settings > Holiday or in the menu bar menu, and a small pixel-art tree stands at the bottom of your screen.

Choose Small, Medium or Large, and drag the tree sideways to where you want it; it stays clear of the Dock and remembers its place on each display. Since version 1.5 you decorate it by choosing from picture tiles: Tree style (Classic, Winter, Cozy or Minimal), Tree topper (Star, Snowflake, Bow or None), Ornaments (Baubles, Sweets, Wooden ornaments or None) and Ornament colors. The tree’s lights follow the palette and animation you chose for the menu bar string; custom colors, holiday styles and sway apply to the string only. More about decorating the tree in Wonderfall 1.5. The Christmas tree guide shows how to add more trees and decorate them right on your desktop.

Can Santa fly across your Mac screen?

Yes. With Santa flyover, Santa and his reindeer cross your screen every so often while you work.

In Settings > Holiday, set Frequency to Sometimes, roughly every 10 to 25 minutes, or Often, roughly every 3 to 8 minutes. The sleigh sound can be turned off, and Call Santa now sends him right away. Santa doesn’t fly while Reduce Motion is on or when a full-screen app covers the display.

How do you let snow pile up and build a snowman?

In Settings > Surface and tools, turn on Snow accumulation: snow settles along the bottom of the screen and on the tops of your open windows.

When the drift gets deep, pick up the Shovel from the menu bar menu or press Control-Option-S, click or drag to dig, and press Esc when you’re done. Cleanup time! sends a small snowplow across the screen instead. To play, choose Play in the snow: roll snowballs along the drift and stack three into a snowman with a beanie and a scarf (the snowman guide shows how). Drifts, window snow and snow play need the Full screen weather area and a Mac that runs Wonderfall’s Metal renderer.

Which ready-made Christmas looks can you copy?

Three combinations make a good start: Quiet focus, Cozy evening and Full Christmas; change any part to make it yours.

  • Quiet focus: a dark wallpaper, Snow on Gentle in the Top strip, Winter Night lights, a Small tree with the Minimal style and Santa flyover set to Off. Festive, with little movement near your work.
  • Cozy evening: a warm, dim wallpaper, Snow on Balanced over windows, Cozy Evening lights and the tree in the Cozy style.
  • Full Christmas: a snowy night wallpaper, Snow on Intense in Full screen with Snow accumulation on, Holiday Lights, a Large tree in the Classic style and Santa set to Often. Good for a break or a holiday video call.

What changes for New Year?

Switch the lights to the New Year scene, and pick the 2027 digit bulbs in the shape picker if you like; the snow and tree can stay as they are.

To make the switch without thinking about it, turn on Switch light styles for holidays (new in 1.5). From December 1 to 26 the lights change to the Velvet Holiday palette with gift bulbs, and from December 27 to January 1 to Midnight Gold with star bulbs, by your Mac’s local time. Only the palette and bulb shape change, and they switch back by themselves when the holiday ends. If you change the scene or palette by hand during a holiday, the holiday style pauses until it is over. After New Year, Winter Night carries the mood through January. The New Year desktop guide covers the 2027 bulbs, snow and what to change after the holidays.

Common questions

Is there a Christmas theme built into macOS?

Not in the wallpapers: Apple’s wallpaper guide for macOS 27 lists no Christmas or holiday wallpaper. Moving snow, lights and a tree come from an app such as Wonderfall.

Does Wonderfall come with Christmas wallpapers?

No. Wonderfall doesn’t install, download or replace wallpapers. Set the picture in System Settings > Wallpaper, and Wonderfall adds the moving decorations on top.

Will snow and lights change my wallpaper?

No. Wonderfall draws on transparent layers above the wallpaper. Turn the effects off, and your desktop is exactly as it was.

Can my own Christmas images fall instead of snowflakes?

Yes. Choose Your image and add up to ten pictures, such as stars, ornaments or a pet in a Santa hat. They can fall like snow, tumble like leaves or drop like rain.

Does it work on a MacBook Air?

Yes, on macOS 14.6 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. Wonderfall lowers its frame rate from 60 to 30 frames per second in Low Power Mode, on Intel Macs and when the Mac is under heavy thermal load.

Where should you start?

Keep the wallpaper you love and build the rest yourself. Get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), turn on Snow, the Holiday Lights scene and the Christmas tree, then decorate the tree your way.
