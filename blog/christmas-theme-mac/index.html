How to Give Your Mac a Christmas Theme: Wallpaper, Snow and Lights

Turn your Mac or MacBook desktop into a Christmas scene: a festive wallpaper, falling snow, menu bar lights, a snow drift and a New Year variation.

By Andrew Sereda, developer of Wonderfall

Get on Mac App Store

To give your Mac a Christmas theme, set a festive wallpaper in System Settings, then add motion on top of it with Wonderfall: falling snow, a string of holiday lights along the menu bar and snow that piles up at the bottom of the screen. The wallpaper sets the colors, and the overlay makes the desktop feel alive without replacing your picture.

A Christmas MacBook wallpaper on its own is a still image. Most of the cozy feeling comes from movement and light: flakes drifting past your windows, bulbs glowing along the top of the screen. This guide puts the pieces together in four steps, with a few ready-made combinations and a New Year variation for the last week of December.

Step 1: Set a Christmas wallpaper on your Mac

  1. Save the image you like. Choose one at least as large as your screen, so a MacBook Pro or an external 4K display does not blur it.
  2. Open System Settings → Wallpaper and choose Add Photo, or right-click the image in Finder and choose Set Desktop Picture.
  3. Prefer a calm picture: a dark evening, a snowy window, a single tree. Busy collages fight with your icons and with any effect you add on top.

Darker wallpapers make white snow and glowing bulbs stand out. On a very bright, white winter picture, snowflakes are harder to see, so choose a larger flake size or a warmer light palette.

Step 2: Add falling snow over the wallpaper

Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store, click its icon in the menu bar and choose Snow. Start with the Gentle or Balanced preset. Pick the area: Top strip keeps snow near the menu bar, Full screen covers everything. Then pick the layer: Over windows to see snow while you work, or Desktop only to keep it behind your apps like a moving wallpaper. Here is how an overlay differs from a live wallpaper or a screensaver.

Step 3: Hang Christmas lights along the menu bar

In Settings, open the lights and turn them on. The Holiday Lights scene gives a warm festive string with little gift-shaped bulbs. Winter Night is cooler and quieter, with pine-tree bulbs, and works for all of January too. The lights have their own layer setting, so they can stay on top while the snow stays behind your windows. The full lights guide covers colors, bulb shapes and glow.

Step 4: Let snow pile up at the bottom of the screen

Under More effects, turn on Snow accumulation. Snow settles along the bottom edge and slowly builds a drift. When it gets too deep, choose Shovel in the menu and dig it out, or use Clear all snow. This needs the Full screen area and a Mac that supports the Metal renderer.

Three ready-made Christmas looks

  • Quiet focus: dark wallpaper, Snow on the Gentle preset in the Top strip, Winter Night lights. Festive, but nothing moves near your work.
  • Cozy evening: warm, dim wallpaper, Snow on Balanced over windows, Cozy Evening lights with their amber candlelight flicker.
  • Full Christmas: snowy night wallpaper, Snow on Intense in Full screen, Snow accumulation on, Holiday Lights. Best for a break or a holiday video call background.

A New Year version for the end of December

After Christmas, switch the lights to the New Year scene: gold bulbs shaped as the digits of 2027, with a slow wave running along the string. For a party mood, Celebration uses star bulbs and a chasing pattern in festive colors. Pair either with a dark new year wallpaper and a gentle snowfall, and change back to Winter Night in January.

Common questions

Is there a Christmas theme built into macOS?

No. macOS lets you change the wallpaper, accent color and icons, but it has no seasonal animations. Snow and lights come from an app such as Wonderfall.

Will snow and lights change my wallpaper?

No. Wonderfall draws on transparent layers above the wallpaper. Turn the effects off, and your desktop is exactly as it was.

Can I use my own Christmas images as falling objects?

Yes. Choose Your image and import up to ten pictures, such as stars, ornaments or a pet in a Santa hat. They can fall like snow, tumble like leaves or drop like rain.

Does it work on a MacBook Air?

Wonderfall runs on macOS 14.6 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. It lowers its frame rate in Low Power Mode or when the Mac gets hot.
