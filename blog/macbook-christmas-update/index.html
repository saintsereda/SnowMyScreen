Is There a MacBook Christmas Update? Where the Snow and Lights in Those Mac Videos Come From

Apple hasn’t released a Christmas update for macOS 27. Where the snow, lights and Santa in Mac videos come from, and how to get the look on your MacBook.

Get on Mac App Store

No. As of November 27, 2026, Apple has not released a Christmas update for the Mac: its pages for macOS 27, the current version, mention no holiday theme, snow mode or festive lights. The snow, lights and Santa you saw in a “MacBook Christmas update” clip come from an app that draws over the desktop, such as Wonderfall from the Mac App Store.

The phrase sounds like something Apple would push to every MacBook in December, so it’s fair to look for it in Software Update. You won’t find it there. This guide shows what Apple’s own pages say, how real macOS updates reach your Mac, why a “Christmas update” offered by a website is a red flag, and how to put snow, lights, a tree and Santa on your own screen in a few minutes.

Is the MacBook Christmas update official?

No: Apple has not announced or released any Christmas, holiday or snow feature for macOS, and its pages for macOS 27 list none.

The current version is macOS 27 Golden Gate. Its page on apple.com and Apple’s support page listing what’s new in each macOS 27 update, up to 27.2.1, talk about Siri, Safari, Shortcuts, photo editing and the refined design. Neither mentions snow, Christmas lights, Santa or a seasonal theme (we checked both on November 27, 2026). Apple’s wallpaper guide has no Christmas wallpaper either, as our Christmas desktop guide notes.

Where do the snow and lights in those Mac videos come from?

From third-party apps: they draw snow, lights and characters on a transparent layer over the desktop, while macOS itself stays exactly as Apple ships it.

If you saw snow falling over open windows, a string of lights along the top of the screen or a sleigh crossing a MacBook display, that’s what such an app does. There are many such apps, and they differ in where the lights hang, whether the snow settles on your windows and whether they add a tree or Santa. A short clip rarely says which app it shows, and we can’t tell you that for any particular video. Our comparison of Christmas apps for Mac covers 28 of them and what each one does.

How do real macOS updates arrive on your Mac?

Only through Software Update in System Settings, so a “Christmas update” offered by a website, a pop-up or a link under a video is not from Apple.

To check for updates on macOS 27, follow Apple’s Software Update guide (checked November 27, 2026):

  1. Open the settings. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, then click General in the sidebar.
  2. Open Software Update. Click Software Update on the right.
  3. Install if there is one. If an update is listed, click Update Now or Update Tonight. If there is no update button, your Mac is up to date.

Fake update pop-ups are a known trick. In November 2023 Malwarebytes reported hacked websites that showed a fake Safari or Chrome update and installed password-stealing malware on Macs. One way to lower the risk is to get decoration apps from the Mac App Store: Apple’s guide to safely opening apps on your Mac explains that Apple reviews the apps in the App Store, and that macOS checks apps from elsewhere before they open.

How do you get snow and Christmas lights on your MacBook?

Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store and turn on Snow, the Holiday Lights scene, a Christmas tree and Santa; it takes about a minute and leaves macOS untouched.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  2. Let it snow. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Season > Snow. In Settings… > Weather you can pick the Gentle, Balanced or Intense preset.
  3. Hang the lights. In Settings > Lights, turn on Light Strip and click the Holiday Lights scene.
  4. Let the lights dress up for the holidays. Turn on Switch light styles for holidays. From December 1 to 26 the bulbs become gifts in the Velvet Holiday palette, and from December 27 to January 1 stars in Midnight Gold; afterwards your own style comes back.
  5. Add a tree. Choose Christmas tree in the menu bar menu. For more trees, open Decoration Inventory and click Place next to the tree.
  6. Call Santa. Choose Call Santa now, and he flies across the screen with his reindeer. In Settings > Holiday, Santa flyover sets how often he comes back on his own: Sometimes, roughly every 10 to 25 minutes, or Often, roughly every 3 to 8.
  7. Keep it in sight while you work. In Settings > Weather, set Display layer to Over windows. The lights have their own display layer in Lights. Your clicks pass straight through.

For snow that piles up, turn on Snow accumulation in Surface and tools: it settles on the tops of your open windows and builds a drift at the bottom of the screen, which you can dig out with the shovel. The Christmas desktop guide has three ready-made looks to copy.

Can Santa and the tree be 3D, or stand on a window?

Yes, since Wonderfall 1.6.1, released on November 23, 2026: Santa, the trees and the other characters can be 3D, and a tree can ride on top of an app window.

  • Pixel or 3D. In Settings > General, Character style switches Santa and his reindeer, the penguins, snowmen, trees, tools and cleaning vehicles between Pixel and 3D at once. New installs start with 3D; if you update from an older version, you keep Pixel until you switch.
  • A tree on a window. Drag a tree and drop its base on the top edge of an app window. It stands there and moves with the window, and it goes back to the bottom of the screen if the window closes, gets covered or becomes too small.
  • Each tree its own way. Right-click a tree from Decoration Inventory to decorate it; the change applies only to that tree.

What’s new in Wonderfall 1.6.1 shows all of it, and the Christmas tree guide covers decorating step by step.

What should you know before you install Wonderfall?

Wonderfall is an app you add, not a change to macOS, so a few things are worth knowing first.

  • It doesn’t touch the system. It draws on transparent layers, keeps your wallpaper and isn’t a screensaver. Turn the effects off, and your desktop is exactly as it was.
  • Lights run along the menu bar. Not around the screen edge, not around windows and not on the Dock.
  • Window snow needs the full screen. Snow on windows, drifts and the shovel need the Full screen weather area and the Metal renderer. If Metal is unavailable on a Mac, snow still falls, but drifts and tools are hidden.
  • Mac only, one price. There is no Windows, iPhone or iPad version. It costs US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store, with no subscription; prices in other regions may differ.

Common questions

Do I need to update macOS to get snow on my Mac?

No. Wonderfall works on macOS 14.6 or later, so it runs on your current system. Updating macOS is still a good idea for security, but it won’t add snow.

Will Apple add a Christmas theme to macOS?

We don’t know. Apple hasn’t announced one, and nothing like it appears in its notes for macOS 27 updates. If it ever comes, it will arrive through Software Update.

Does it work on a MacBook Air?

Yes, on macOS 14.6 or later. In Low Power Mode, on Intel Macs and when the Mac is under heavy thermal load, Wonderfall lowers its frame rate from 60 to 30 frames per second.

Can I turn it off after Christmas?

Yes. Turn off the weather effects and lights in the menu bar menu, or switch to another season: Wonderfall also has rain, autumn leaves, spring petals and summer fireflies.

Where should you start?

There is no Christmas update to wait for, so make your own. Get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store, choose Season > Snow, turn on the Holiday Lights scene and press Call Santa now. Then drop a tree on top of your browser window and see if anyone asks where you got the update.
