Cozy Winter Desktop for Mac: Snow and Warm Lights Over Your Own Wallpaper

Make a cozy winter desktop on your Mac without a new wallpaper: warm Cozy Evening lights, gentle snow behind your windows and three recipes to start from.

Get on Mac App Store

A cozy winter desktop doesn’t need a new wallpaper. Keep the one you like and add the moving part on top: in Wonderfall, choose the Cozy Evening scene in Lights, Snow with the Gentle preset in Weather, and Desktop only as the display layer. Soft snow falls behind your windows, warm lights glow along the menu bar, and your wallpaper stays as it is.

A good winter wallpaper sets the scene, and you can keep using the one you love. What it can’t do is move. This guide shows how to add that layer yourself with Wonderfall: a setup in six steps, three cozy recipes to start from, and a few macOS settings that make the whole Mac feel warmer in the evening.

Is there a cozy animated winter background for Mac?

Not built in: macOS has no wallpaper with falling snow, so the moving part has to come from an app that draws on top of your wallpaper.

In System Settings > Wallpaper, Apple offers Dynamic Wallpapers, aerial landscapes and cityscapes, colors and your own photos, but no falling snow and no winter or holiday category. Wonderfall adds snow and lights on transparent layers above whatever picture you choose. Here is what each piece does:

A winter wallpaper aloneYour wallpaper with a Wonderfall layer
What movesA photo stays still, and none of Apple’s wallpapers, Dynamic Wallpapers included, has falling snowFalling snow, and lights that twinkle, breathe or glow steadily along the menu bar
Who picks the moodThe photographer or artist who made itYou: 8 light scenes, 12 palettes, your own light colors, three snow presets or your own settings
While you workSeen only where windows don’t cover the desktopBehind your windows with Desktop only, or in front of them with Over windows
Changing itFind and download another picturePick another scene or preset in Settings

The wallpaper details come from Apple’s Mac User Guide, checked on November 25, 2026. Wonderfall never replaces the wallpaper, so any winter photo of your own works as the base.

How do I make my Mac desktop cozy for winter?

Pick a wallpaper you like, then add warm lights and gentle snow in Wonderfall. It takes six steps.

  1. Choose the base. In System Settings > Wallpaper, pick a winter photo of your own or any wallpaper with calm colors. Wonderfall draws on top of it.
  2. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  3. Open Settings. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Settings…
  4. Turn on warm lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and click Cozy Evening under Scenes.
  5. Add soft snow. In Weather, choose Snow and the Gentle preset.
  6. Put it behind your work. Set the weather’s Display layer to Desktop only, so the snow stays behind open windows like part of the wallpaper.

That’s the cozy base. The recipes below change it into three different moods.

What cozy winter setups can you make on a Mac?

Three combinations cover most of what people mean by a cozy winter desktop: a warm evening, a snowy winter wonderland and a quiet setup for focused work.

1. Cozy evening: warm lights and gentle snow

  • Lights: the Cozy Evening scene. For a slower glow, change the animation to Candlelight or Breathe, and lower Brightness a little.
  • Snow: the Gentle preset, so a few flakes drift down instead of a storm.
  • Wallpaper: an evening photo with warm tones, such as a lit window or a street at dusk.

2. Winter wonderland: Winter Night lights and snow drifts

  • Lights: the Winter Night scene.
  • Snow: the Balanced preset with the Full screen weather area. In Surface and tools, turn on Snow accumulation, and snow settles along the bottom of the screen and builds a bank over time. Pick up the shovel to dig it out, or choose Cleanup time! to send a snowplow across.
  • A tree: in Holiday, keep Christmas tree on (it is on by default) and choose the Winter tree style: a snowflake on top and blue and silver baubles. It stands on the snow pile. The Christmas tree guide covers the rest.
  • A snowman, if you like: choose Play in the snow, roll three balls and stack them. How to build a snowman on your Mac desktop.

Snow drifts and the shovel need the Full screen area and a Mac that runs Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. Where Metal isn’t available, snow still falls, but the drifts and tools are hidden.

3. Quiet focus: your own warm colors, behind your work

  • Lights: open the palette’s Customize panel and turn on Use custom colors, then pick one to six warm colors, such as amber and soft gold, with the standard macOS color picker. Set the animation to Steady or Slow wave.
  • Snow: the Gentle preset with the Top strip weather area, so flakes fall only near the menu bar.
  • Layer: Desktop only for the weather. The lights have their own Display layer too.

Every scene is a starting point: after you click one, you can still change the palette, bulb shape, animation, bulb size, spacing, brightness and glow. The lights guide explains every option.

How do you keep a cozy desktop from getting in the way?

Put the snow behind your windows with Desktop only, keep the lights’ Hide lights on hover on, and use Pause all effects when you need a plain screen.

  • Desktop only or Over windows. Desktop only keeps the effect behind open windows, so a maximized window hides it. Over windows keeps it in view while you work. You can switch either one from the Weather display and Lights display submenus without opening Settings.
  • Clicks pass through. Snow and lights ignore the mouse, so clicks go to your apps and desktop icons.
  • Hide lights on hover is on by default: the bulbs fade when the pointer is over them.
  • Reduce motion. With Reduce motion turned on in System Settings > Accessibility > Motion, the lights glow steadily instead of animating.
  • Pause all effects in the Wonderfall menu stops everything at once, and the same item brings it back.
  • Launch at login in Settings > General starts Wonderfall when you sign in, so the cozy desktop is there every morning.

How do I make my Mac feel cozy beyond the desktop?

Three built-in macOS settings help: Night Shift for warmer screen colors in the evening, Dark Mode, and a desktop without files on it, so the wallpaper and the snow have room.

  • Night Shift. In System Settings > Displays, click Night Shift and choose a schedule: Sunset to Sunrise or your own hours. The screen shifts to warmer colors at that time (Apple’s Night Shift guide). Sunset to Sunrise needs Location Services.
  • Dark Mode. In System Settings > Appearance, choose Dark for a dark appearance (Apple’s appearance guide).
  • A clear desktop. In System Settings > Desktop & Dock, turn off On Desktop under Show Items, the option Apple describes as showing items on the desktop (Apple’s Desktop & Dock guide).

These steps follow Apple’s Mac User Guide for macOS 27, checked on November 25, 2026; older versions of macOS may name them a little differently.

Can a winter desktop stay after Christmas?

Yes. Snow, Winter Night and Cozy Evening aren’t tied to Christmas, so the same setup works from December through February.

If you turned on Switch light styles for holidays in Lights, the lights change to a Christmas style from December 1 to 26 and a New Year style from December 27 to January 1, then return to your own palette and shape by themselves. The tree goes away with the same Christmas tree item in the Wonderfall menu. For New Year’s Eve itself, the New Year desktop guide shows the New Year scene and the 2027 bulbs.

To let the weather decide, turn on Automatically match local weather in Weather. It uses Apple WeatherKit and your approximate location: snow on snowy days, rain on rainy ones, and in the Northern Hemisphere from December to February snow on the other days too. It needs internet access and the location permission, which Wonderfall asks for only when you turn the option on.

What doesn’t Wonderfall add to a cozy desktop?

Wonderfall doesn’t include wallpapers or sounds, and it isn’t a screen saver.

  • No wallpapers. It doesn’t install, download or replace wallpapers. The picture underneath is yours.
  • No fireplace sounds or music. The effects are silent. Only the cleaning tools, snow play and Santa’s optional sleigh sound make any sound.
  • Not a screen saver. It works on your desktop while you use your Mac and doesn’t replace the macOS screen saver. The Christmas screensaver guide covers that side.
  • Lights along the menu bar only. The string doesn’t run around the screen edge or along the Dock.

Common questions

Will Wonderfall replace my wallpaper?

No. It draws snow and lights on transparent layers above your wallpaper. Turn the effects off, and your desktop looks exactly as before.

Does it work on a MacBook Air?

Yes, with macOS 14.6 or later. Wonderfall runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs, and it lowers its frame rate from 60 to 30 frames per second in Low Power Mode, on Intel Macs and when the Mac is under heavy thermal load.

Can I use my own pictures instead of snowflakes?

Yes. Choose Your image and add up to ten pictures, such as stars or tiny mittens, and they fall instead of snow. Pictures with transparent backgrounds look best, and they stay on your Mac.

Where should you start?

Get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), click Cozy Evening in Lights and set the snow to Gentle. Live with it for an evening, then make it yours: a different scene, your own colors, or a snow bank with a tree on top.
