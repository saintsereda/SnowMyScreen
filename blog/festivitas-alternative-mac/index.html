Festivitas Alternatives for Mac in 2026: Christmas Lights, Snow and More

Festivitas is not on the Mac App Store. Compare six alternatives for Christmas lights and snow, see which are free, and how to switch to Wonderfall.

Get on Mac App Store

A good Festivitas alternative from the Mac App Store is Wonderfall: it hangs animated lights along the menu bar and adds snow that settles on your windows and piles up at the bottom of the screen, a Christmas tree and Santa, for a one-time US$3.99. Festivitas lights the menu bar and the Dock, added snow in 2025, and its Mac version is sold only on the developer’s website.

People look for a Festivitas alternative for different reasons. Some prefer to buy and update apps through the Mac App Store, some want lights that come with a whole winter scene, and some just want to see what else is out there before December. This guide sums up what Festivitas does and how it is sold, then compares it with six other holiday apps for Mac, using the developers’ websites and App Store listings as they stood on 21 November 2026.

What does Festivitas do?

Festivitas is a holiday app by Simon Stovring that hangs animated string lights along the menu bar and the Dock of your Mac and, since its 2025 update, adds falling snow.

According to its website, the lights sparkle and react to your mouse, you can change the lights, colors and effects, and the Mac app has Shortcuts actions for the lights and snow. When MacRumors covered the 2025 holiday update on 25 November 2025, it reported that the snow parts around the cursor, spans all your displays and has settings for amount, flake size, fall speed and wind, alongside new light shapes, color presets and animation patterns. The same update brought a separate iPhone and iPad app with Home Screen widgets and photo frames decorated with lights. The developer’s site doesn’t say whether the snow falls in front of your windows or only on the desktop, and it lists no Christmas tree or Santa.

Is Festivitas on the Mac App Store?

No. The Mac version of Festivitas is sold on the developer’s website through Gumroad; a search of the Mac App Store on 21 November 2026 found no Mac listing; the App Store has only the iPhone and iPad app.

The developer’s FAQ on festivitas.app explains why: apps that use accessibility APIs can’t be distributed through the Mac App Store, and Festivitas uses them to find and decorate the Dock. That is also why it asks for Accessibility access, which you manage in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility. You choose how much to pay for the Mac app, above a minimum price set in euros, and pay once.

Wonderfall, by contrast, is in the Mac App Store: you buy it with your Apple Account, and updates come through the App Store like any other app.

Is there a free alternative to Festivitas?

Yes, some of the apps in the table cost nothing to download: Snowy! is free, while Festive Lights – Mood Lights and Desktop Lights are free to start and sell a Pro upgrade or light collections inside the app.

Wonderfall is a paid app: US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store, with no subscription and no in-app purchases (prices in other regions may differ). That one purchase covers the lights, snow, tree and Santa for December, and rain, autumn leaves, spring petals and summer fireflies for the rest of the year, so the app keeps working long after the holidays.

Why choose Wonderfall as a Festivitas alternative?

Choose Wonderfall if you want animated lights along the menu bar plus a whole winter scene on your desktop, from an app you buy once in the Mac App Store.

The lights are separate from the snow, so you can use either or both. In Settings › Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick one of eight scenes, such as Holiday Lights, Winter Night or New Year. Then change anything: 12 color palettes, 20 bulb shapes including stars, gifts, hearts and pine trees, 10 animations such as Twinkle, Chase and Candlelight, plus bulb size, spacing, brightness and glow. Hide lights on hover fades the bulbs when your pointer passes over them.

Version 1.5 added three things for the holidays:

  • Sway with weather: the string swings in the wind of the snow falling on your screen, and Wind sensitivity sets how much it moves.
  • Use custom colors: pick one to six colors of your own in the standard macOS color picker.
  • Switch light styles for holidays: from 1 to 26 December the lights switch to the Velvet Holiday palette with gift-shaped bulbs, from 27 December to 1 January to Midnight Gold with stars, and then back to your own settings.

All three are off until you turn them on. The Christmas lights guide walks through every setting.

How is Wonderfall’s snow different?

Wonderfall’s snow doesn’t only fall: with Snow accumulation on, it builds a drift along the bottom of the screen and settles on the top edges of your open windows, and you can dig it out, play with it or clear it in one go.

Pick from ten flake shapes and the Gentle, Balanced or Intense preset, or tune intensity, flake size, speed and wind by hand. Choose Over windows to keep the snow in front of your apps while you work, or Desktop only to keep it behind them, in front of your own wallpaper. Either way, clicks pass straight through to your apps and desktop icons. When the snow shows over your windows, it rides along on their top edges, and a quick drag makes it slide off.

  • Shovel. Pick it up from the menu bar menu or press Control-Option-S, then click or drag to dig the drift out. Esc puts it back.
  • Cleanup time! sends a small snowplow, or another vehicle you choose, across the bottom of the screen.
  • Play in the snow. Roll snowballs along the drift, stack three into a snowman that gets a beanie and a scarf, and throw snowballs that splat on the screen. The snowman guide shows how.

Can a Festivitas alternative add a Christmas tree and Santa?

Wonderfall can: Settings › Holiday has a small decorated pixel-art Christmas tree for your desktop and a setting for how often Santa and his reindeer fly across the screen.

The tree comes in Small, Medium or Large. Drag it sideways along the bottom of the screen; it keeps clear of the Dock, and its lights follow the palette and animation of your menu bar lights. Since version 1.5 you can decorate it with a Tree style (Classic, Winter, Cozy or Minimal) or choose the topper, ornaments and ornament colors yourself (what’s new in Wonderfall 1.5).

Set Santa flyover to Sometimes (roughly every 10 to 25 minutes) or Often (roughly every 3 to 8 minutes). Santa sound, on by default, plays his sleigh, and Call Santa now, also in the menu bar menu, brings him over straight away. He skips his flight while Reduce Motion is on or when a full-screen app covers the display.

What does Festivitas do that Wonderfall doesn’t?

Wonderfall’s lights hang only along the menu bar: it can’t light up the Dock or run bulbs around the edge of the screen, which Festivitas and some of the other apps in the table do.

It also has no Shortcuts actions and no schedule, so you can’t start the lights or snow at a set time. The only date-based change is Switch light styles for holidays, which changes the palette and bulb shape on set dates. And Wonderfall is a Mac app only, with no iPhone or iPad version.

On permissions, Wonderfall’s keyboard shortcuts need no Accessibility access, snow on windows needs no Screen Recording permission because the app reads only window positions, and it asks for your location only if you turn on Match local weather.

How do you switch from Festivitas to Wonderfall?

Quit Festivitas, install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store and turn on the lights, snow, tree and Santa in its Settings window, which opens from the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar.

  1. Quit Festivitas, so you don’t end up with two strings of lights along the menu bar. If you stop using it, you can also remove it from System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.
  2. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  3. Hang the lights. Click the Wonderfall icon, choose Settings…, open Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick the Holiday Lights scene.
  4. Turn on snow. In Weather, choose Snow and the Gentle or Balanced preset, then pick Over windows or Desktop only.
  5. Let it pile up. In Surface and tools, turn on Snow accumulation.
  6. Add the tree and Santa. In Holiday, keep Christmas tree on, choose its size and set Santa flyover to Sometimes or Often.

To have it all back after a restart, turn on Launch at login in General. For more ideas, read how to give your Mac a Christmas theme.

If you came looking for a Festivitas alternative you can buy once in the Mac App Store, get Wonderfall, turn on Holiday Lights and snow, and let the first drift build at the bottom of your screen while you work.
