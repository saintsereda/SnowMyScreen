How to Put a Christmas Tree on Your Mac Desktop (and Let Santa Fly By)

Put an animated Christmas tree on your Mac desktop, stand it on top of a window, give each tree its own decorations in Wonderfall 1.6.1 and let Santa fly by.

Get on Mac App Store

To put a Christmas tree on your Mac desktop, install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store and turn on Christmas tree in its menu bar menu or in Settings > Holiday. A decorated tree with glowing lights appears at the bottom of your screen, in front of your own wallpaper. Drag it where you like, even onto the top of a window, decorate it and let Santa fly past.

Wonderfall is a menu bar app that draws winter decorations on transparent layers above your desktop, so the tree isn’t a wallpaper or a screensaver: it stands there while you work. This guide was updated on November 27, 2026 for Wonderfall 1.6.1, the current version, which lets a tree stand on top of an app window, gives every tree its own decorations and adds 3D artwork.

How do you put a Christmas tree on your Mac desktop?

Turn on Christmas tree in Wonderfall, and a decorated tree appears at the bottom of your screen right away.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  2. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and make sure Christmas tree is checked. The tree is on by default, so it may already be standing at the bottom of your screen.
  3. Choose a size. Open Settings… from the same menu, go to Holiday and pick Small, Medium or Large.
  4. Move it. Drag the tree sideways along the bottom of the screen, or drop it on the top edge of an app window. The first time you hover over it, it shows “Drag to move · Right-click to decorate”.

The same Christmas tree item in the menu bar menu takes the tree away again, without opening Settings.

Is the tree animated, or is it a wallpaper?

It’s animated: the tree’s lights glow and move with the animation you choose, on a transparent layer above your wallpaper, so your own background stays exactly as it is and the tree stands in front of it.

The tree’s lights follow the palette, animation, brightness and speed you set for Wonderfall’s menu bar lights in Settings > Lights, so one change there recolors both the string along the top of the screen and the tree at the bottom. Custom colors, holiday styles and sway apply to the menu bar string only, not to the tree. The Christmas lights guide explains the palettes and animations.

The tree comes in two looks. In Settings > General, Character style switches it between Pixel and 3D, together with Santa, the penguins, snowmen, tools and cleaning vehicles. New installs start with 3D; if you updated from version 1.6, you keep Pixel until you switch.

Because Wonderfall isn’t a screensaver, the tree doesn’t wait for your Mac to go idle: it stays on your desktop while you work, as long as the tree and Wonderfall’s effects are on. The Christmas screensaver guide explains the difference if you’re choosing between the two.

How do you decorate the Christmas tree?

Right-click the tree on your desktop and choose from the picture tiles, or decorate the main tree in Settings > Holiday; every change shows on the tree right away.

There are four groups of choices, added in Wonderfall 1.5:

  • Tree style: Classic (star, baubles, red and gold), Winter (snowflake, baubles, blue and silver), Cozy (bow, sweets, colorful) or Minimal (no topper and no ornaments). A style sets the three groups below in one click.
  • Tree topper: Star (the default), Snowflake, Bow or None.
  • Ornaments: Baubles, Sweets, Wooden ornaments or None (the default).
  • Ornament colors: Red and gold (the default), Blue and silver, or Colorful. These are three fixed sets rather than a free color picker, and they’re unavailable while Ornaments is set to None.

The tree starts with a star and no ornaments, which matches none of the four styles, so no style tile is selected until you pick one.

Right-click or Control-click a tree on your desktop, and a Christmas tree window opens with a preview, a Size menu and the same four groups. Since version 1.6.1 that window changes only the tree you opened it for, so every tree can look different; the main tree still uses the choices in Settings > Holiday. A plain drag still moves the tree. The Wonderfall 1.5 release post shows the decorations when they first arrived.

Can you put more than one Christmas tree on your desktop?

Yes, since Wonderfall 1.6: open Decoration Inventory from the Wonderfall menu and click Place next to Christmas tree, or drag the tree from that window onto your desktop.

  1. Open the inventory. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Decoration Inventory.
  2. Place a tree. Click Place next to Christmas tree, and a new tree appears on the display under your pointer. You can also drag the tree out of the window and drop it on the desktop.
  3. Arrange the trees. New trees take turns among five preset spots along the bottom; drag each one sideways, or onto a window top, to where you want it.
  4. Decorate, resize or remove one. Right-click a placed tree to give it its own decorations, change its size or choose Return to Inventory to take it away.

Each placed tree stands on the bottom of the screen, keeps clear of the Dock like the main tree, and is remembered the next time Wonderfall starts. Since version 1.6.1, each placed tree also keeps its own decorations, and trees you placed in 1.6 keep the look they had. Decoration Inventory is unavailable while effects are paused. Can you put a Christmas tree on top of a window?

Yes, since Wonderfall 1.6.1: drag the main tree or a placed tree and drop its base on the top edge of an ordinary app window, and it stands there and moves with the window.

If the window closes, gets covered or becomes too small, the tree goes back to the bottom of the screen. While it rides on a window, the tree is drawn above the window’s content even when the Weather display layer is set to Desktop only. Floating panels and menus don’t hold trees. A finished snowman can stand on a window the same way. What’s new in Wonderfall 1.6.1 shows trees and snowmen on windows.

Where does the tree stand, and can you see it over your windows?

The tree stands at the bottom of the screen or on top of a window, moves sideways along the bottom and keeps clear of the Dock; whether you see it over your windows depends on the Weather Display layer.

The tree stays on screen and remembers its place on each display. With the Dock at the bottom, it sits above the Dock where the two overlap, and if snow has piled up, it stands on top of the drift. The layer is set in Settings > Weather under Display layer, and the tree follows the same layer as the weather. How do you make Santa fly across your Mac screen?

Set Santa flyover in Settings > Holiday to Sometimes or Often, and Santa and his reindeer cross your screen every so often while you work.

  • Frequency: Off, Sometimes (the default, roughly every 10 to 25 minutes) or Often (roughly every 3 to 8 minutes).
  • Santa sound: a sleigh sound as he flies past, on by default. Turn it off for a silent Santa.
  • Call Santa now: sends him across right away. It’s also in the Wonderfall menu in the menu bar.

With the 3D character style, Santa and the reindeer are drawn in 3D. Wonderfall picks the direction and the display for each flight. Santa doesn’t fly when Reduce motion is on in System Settings > Accessibility > Motion, when effects are turned off on that display, or when a full-screen app covers it.

How do you stand the tree in the snow?

Turn on Snow in Settings > Weather, then Snow accumulation in Surface and tools: a drift builds along the bottom of the screen, and the tree stands on top of it.

The faster the snow falls, the faster the drift grows. When it gets deep, pick up the Shovel from the Wonderfall menu or press Control-Option-S, click or drag to dig, and press Esc when you’re done; Cleanup time! sends a small snowplow across the screen instead. Drifts need the Full screen weather area and a Mac that runs Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. Next to the tree you can build a snowman, or choose Call Penguins and watch three penguins slide across the snow.

Common questions

Does the Christmas tree replace my wallpaper?

No. Wonderfall draws the tree on a transparent layer above your wallpaper. Turn the tree off, and your desktop is exactly as it was.

Does the tree need snow?

No. The tree stands at the bottom of the screen whether or not snow is falling. With Snow accumulation on, it stands on top of the drift.

Can I decorate each tree differently?

Yes, since version 1.6.1. Right-click a tree you placed from Decoration Inventory, and the changes apply to that tree only. The main tree uses the choices in Settings > Holiday.

Can I choose any color for the ornaments?

No. Ornaments come in three fixed sets: Red and gold, Blue and silver, and Colorful. The tree’s lights follow the palette of your menu bar lights, so changing that palette in Lights recolors them.

Does Santa make a sound?

Yes, a sleigh sound, which is on by default. Turn off Santa sound in Settings > Holiday if you’d rather he flew past quietly.

Where should you start?

Start with the tree itself: get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), make sure Christmas tree is checked in its menu, then right-click the tree and pick the Classic style. Add a second tree from Decoration Inventory in the Winter style, stand it on top of your browser window, set Santa to Sometimes, and your desktop is ready for December.
