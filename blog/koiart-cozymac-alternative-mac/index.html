KoiArt vs CozyMac vs Wonderfall: Snow Apps for Mac Compared

KoiArt (US$9) and CozyMac (US$9.99) aren’t in the US Mac App Store. Compare them with Wonderfall, the US$3.99 App Store alternative, checked November 2026.

Get on Mac App Store

KoiArt and CozyMac are snow apps for Mac sold on their developers’ own websites, for US$9 and US$9.99 for one computer. Wonderfall is the alternative you get from the Mac App Store, for a one-time US$3.99. All three let snow fall and pile up on your windows; Wonderfall adds menu bar lights with eight scenes, a rack of hand tools, a snowman wardrobe, your own falling images and penguins.

If you searched for KoiArt or CozyMac, you probably want snow on your Mac desktop this December and are deciding which app to trust with it. This guide explains what each of the two does and how it is sold, compares them with Wonderfall feature by feature, and shows how to set up Wonderfall in a few minutes. Facts about KoiArt and CozyMac come from their own websites, checked on November 22, 2026.

What does KoiArt do?

KoiArt is a menu bar app by Spacerr LLC that puts animated weather and Christmas decorations over your screen and also sets painted wallpapers, on Mac and on Windows PCs.

According to its website, KoiArt has six effects: snow, leaves, starfall, rain, sakura and fireflies. Snow piles on window tops and the Dock and slides off when you drag a window, and its snow page adds that sweeping the pointer through the snow rolls snowballs you can stack into a snowman, while a snowplow clears deep snow. Christmas items include up to three trees, snowmen, Santa, a yeti and a little gift train. It needs macOS 14 or later, and a one-time license on the website costs US$9 for one computer (there is also a license for five); you download the app from there too.

What does CozyMac do?

CozyMac (also written Cozy Mac) is a Mac app that makes it snow or rain on your desktop, lets snow pile up on windows, follows the forecast for a city you pick and comes with 4K wallpapers.

Its website lists ten weather scenes, among them snow, rain, storm, leaves, petals, fireflies, meteors and mist. From the menu bar you can call Santa, bury the desktop in snow for a snowplow to dig out, freeze the screen and scrape the ice, light a fireplace that crackles and melts the snow, or start a light show on your window tops. Snow piles on windows, folders and the Dock, and holding Option while you sweep rolls snowballs for a snowman. It needs macOS 14 or later and costs US$9.99, one-time, for one Mac, paid through a link on the site.

Are KoiArt and CozyMac on the Mac App Store?

Not as far as we could find: both are downloaded from their developers’ websites and unlocked with a license key sent by email, and a search of the US Mac App Store on November 22, 2026 found neither app.

Wonderfall is on the Mac App Store. You buy it with your Apple Account, there is no license key to keep, and updates arrive through the App Store like any other app. It needs no account or sign-in. It also costs less: US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store, compared with US$9 for KoiArt and US$9.99 for CozyMac for one computer on their websites, checked on November 22, 2026.

All three draw on a transparent layer over your screen, let clicks pass through and let snow settle on your windows; they differ in where you get them, what you can do with the snow and what comes with it.

What does Wonderfall do that KoiArt and CozyMac don’t?

Wonderfall is the one of the three you can get from the Mac App Store, and it adds things neither website lists: a string of lights along the menu bar, a wardrobe for your snowman, your own images falling like snow, and penguins. It also gives you a rack of tools to pick up: a shovel, a squeegee and a broom.

  • Menu bar lights. In Settings > Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick one of eight scenes, such as Holiday Lights or Winter Night, then change the palette, bulb shape, animation, brightness and glow. Switch light styles for holidays can turn the string Christmas-colored from December 1 by itself. The Christmas lights guide covers every setting.
  • Tools in your hand. The tool for the current weather stands on a small rack at the bottom of the screen: a shovel for snow, a squeegee for raindrops or a broom for leaves and petals. Pick it up with a click or Control-Option-S, use it, and press Esc to put it back.
  • A Snowman Wardrobe. Click a snowman you built to give it a beanie, a top hat or earmuffs, a smile or a wink, coal or wooden buttons, and a scarf, a bow tie or a winter cape. Make Default dresses every new snowman the same way.
  • Your own images. Choose Your image, add up to ten pictures, and they fall like snow, tumble like leaves or drop like rain.
  • Penguins. Choose Call Penguins in the Wonderfall menu, and three penguins run in, belly-slide across the snow, wave and run off.

How does Wonderfall’s snow work?

Wonderfall’s snow falls, piles up and can be cleaned or played with: turn on Snow accumulation in Settings > Surface and tools, and a drift builds along the bottom of the screen and on the top edges of your open windows.

Choose from ten flake shapes and the Gentle, Balanced or Intense preset, or tune intensity, flake size, speed and wind yourself. Put the snow Over windows to see it while you work or Desktop only to keep it behind your apps. When the drift gets deep, dig with the shovel, or choose Cleanup time! to send a snowplow or one of three other vehicles across the screen. Choose Play in the snow or press Control-Option-P to roll snowballs and stack a snowman. Snow on windows reads only where your windows are, never what’s in them, so Wonderfall needs no Screen Recording permission. The snow guide explains every control.

Does Wonderfall have a Christmas tree, Santa and other visitors?

Yes. A small decorated pixel-art Christmas tree stands on your desktop, Santa and his reindeer fly across the screen every so often, and since version 1.6 penguins come when you call them.

Decorate the tree in Settings > Holiday or by right-clicking it: Tree style (Classic, Winter, Cozy or Minimal), Tree topper, Ornaments and Ornament colors. Decoration Inventory in the Wonderfall menu places more trees; all of them share one decoration. Set Santa flyover to Sometimes, roughly every 10 to 25 minutes, or Often, roughly every 3 to 8, or click Call Santa now. The Christmas tree guide covers both step by step.

What do KoiArt and CozyMac include that Wonderfall leaves out?

Wallpapers and a few extra scenes: KoiArt and CozyMac both come with their own wallpapers, KoiArt also runs on Windows, and CozyMac has sound effects such as a crackling fireplace and a light show that flashes to music.

Wonderfall doesn’t install or replace wallpapers; it decorates the wallpaper you already have. Its lights hang only along the menu bar, not on window tops, and it isn’t a screensaver; KoiArt also comes with a Christmas screen saver, and CozyMac runs its light show on window tops. It is a Mac app only, and it has no ambient sound: only the hand tools, snow play and Santa’s sleigh make a sound, and the sleigh can be turned off. It also has no starfall, storm or mist scenes. What it does have is a complete winter and holiday scene in one app from the Mac App Store, plus rain, leaves, petals and fireflies for the rest of the year.

How do you switch to Wonderfall?

Quit KoiArt or CozyMac so you don’t get two layers of snow, install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store and turn on snow, lights and the tree in its Settings window.

  1. Quit the other app from its menu bar icon.
  2. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  3. Turn on snow. Click the Wonderfall icon, choose Settings…, open Weather, choose Snow and the Gentle or Balanced preset.
  4. Let it pile up. In Surface and tools, turn on Snow accumulation.
  5. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick Holiday Lights.
  6. Add the tree and Santa. In Holiday, keep Christmas tree on and keep Santa flyover on Sometimes.

Turn on Launch at login in General to have it all back after a restart. If you came here comparing KoiArt and CozyMac, get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store for a one-time US$3.99, turn on the snow and the Holiday Lights, and pick up the shovel when the first drift builds.
