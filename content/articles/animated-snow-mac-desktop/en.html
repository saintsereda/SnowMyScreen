There are three ways to get animated snow on a Mac: a live wallpaper app that plays a snowy video behind your windows, a snow screensaver that runs only while the Mac is idle, or a desktop overlay that draws snow over your own wallpaper. macOS has no snow wallpaper of its own. Only an overlay keeps snow falling while you work, and Wonderfall can also let it pile up.

Search for a snow live wallpaper for Mac and you will find all three mixed together. They look alike in a preview, but they behave very differently once you sit down to work. This guide explains what macOS offers on its own, what each option does, where it falls short and how to pick the one that fits your day. It was updated on November 27, 2026, with Apple’s pages for macOS 27 and Wonderfall 1.6.1.

Does macOS have an animated snow wallpaper?

No: macOS 27 has dynamic and aerial wallpapers, but none with snow, and Apple doesn’t describe a setting for video or live wallpapers.

According to Apple’s wallpaper guide (checked November 27, 2026), Dynamic Wallpapers brighten and darken with the time of day where you are, and the aerial wallpapers of landscapes, cities, underwater scenes and Earth animate as you lock or unlock your Mac. Most of the rest are still pictures, colors and your own photos, plus an animated gradient that spells “Mac”. There is no winter or holiday category, so falling snow has to come from an app.

What is a snow live wallpaper on a Mac?

It is a third-party app that plays a video, such as a snowy forest, in place of your desktop picture, so the snow is part of the background itself.

This works if you want the whole background to be a winter scene. The trade-off is that you give up your own wallpaper for as long as the video plays, and the snow disappears wherever a window covers the desktop. A live wallpaper also stays a picture: snow in the video doesn’t land on your windows or build up at the bottom of the screen.

Is a snow screensaver the same thing?

No: a screensaver appears only after your Mac has been idle for a while and stops the moment you touch the keyboard or trackpad.

Apple’s screen saver guide puts the settings in System Settings > Wallpaper > Screen Saver and says it starts after the idle time you choose. The built-in choices are macOS, the aerials, Mac, Shuffle Aerials and Other, which includes your own photos; none of them shows snow. A snowfall screensaver can look lovely on a Mac you aren’t using, but it does nothing while you work, which is when most people want a little winter atmosphere. If you download a screen saver file, use a source you trust, because it is software that runs on your Mac. The Christmas screensaver guide shows how to make one from your own photos instead.

What does a desktop snow overlay do?

An overlay app draws snow on a separate, transparent layer above your wallpaper, so your background stays exactly as it is and clicks pass straight through to your apps.

Wonderfall is an overlay app for the Mac, and the snow apps comparison shows how other overlay apps handle snow. In Wonderfall you choose two things in Settings > Weather:

  • Display layer. Desktop only keeps the snow behind your windows, like a moving wallpaper; Over windows lets it fall in front of them while you work.
  • Weather area. Full screen fills the display; Top strip keeps the snow in the top 150 points, near the menu bar.

Three presets, Gentle, Balanced and Intense, set the snowfall in one click, and Custom adds sliders for intensity, flake size, speed and wind. Turn on Automatically match local weather, and Wonderfall shows snow when it is snowing where you are. On top of the snow you can build a snowman, put up a Christmas tree or call penguins.

How do you set up animated snow over your own wallpaper?

Install Wonderfall, choose Snow and set the display layer; it takes about a minute.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later.
  2. Turn on snow. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Season > Snow.
  3. Choose where it falls. In Settings… > Weather, set Display layer to Desktop only for a wallpaper-like effect, or Over windows to see the snow in front of your apps.
  4. Tune it. Start with the Gentle preset for a calm background, or Intense for a proper snowstorm, then fine-tune in Custom.

Can the snow pile up and settle on your windows?

Yes, in Wonderfall: turn on Snow accumulation in Settings > Surface and tools, and snow gathers on the tops of your open windows and builds a drift along the bottom of the screen.

The snow on a window moves with it and slides off when you drag the window quickly. When the drift gets deep, pick up the Shovel from the menu bar menu or press Control-Option-S and dig, or choose Cleanup time! to send a small snowplow across the screen. Since version 1.6.1, a snowman or a Christmas tree can stand on a window top too. Window snow and drifts need the Full screen weather area and Wonderfall’s Metal renderer; very small windows don’t collect snow.

What else do people ask about snow on a Mac?

A few short answers about how an overlay behaves day to day.

Does a snow overlay replace my wallpaper?

No. The overlay is a separate transparent layer. Your wallpaper, desktop icons and windows stay where they are, and turning the effect off leaves the desktop exactly as it was.

Will the snow get in the way of my clicks?

No. Wonderfall’s layers ignore mouse clicks, so everything underneath keeps working.

Can I use my own pictures instead of snowflakes?

Yes. Choose Your image and add up to ten pictures, such as gingerbread men or stars. They can fall like snow, tumble like leaves or drop like rain.

Does it work with more than one display?

Yes. Wonderfall shows effects on every connected display, and Settings > General > Displays turns each one on or off.

Will animated snow slow down my Mac?

Wonderfall draws with Metal and lowers its frame rate from 60 to 30 frames per second in Low Power Mode, on Intel Macs and when the Mac is under heavy thermal load. Pause all effects in the menu bar menu stops everything for a while.

Where should you start?

If you want snow you can see while you work, an overlay is the one to try. Get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), choose Season > Snow with the Desktop only layer, and keep your favorite wallpaper underneath. When you want more, add lights and a tree with the Christmas desktop guide.
