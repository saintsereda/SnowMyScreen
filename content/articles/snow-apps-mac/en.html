Which Mac snow apps pile snow on your windows?

Nine of the eleven apps in the table say their snow settles on the tops of your windows (SnowWall only on the front window); Let It Snow and Let It Snow Desktop describe snow that falls behind your windows instead.

The apps that settle snow also say what happens when you move a window. In KoiArt, Snowhush and Wonderfall, dragging a window makes its snow slide off; in Snow Now and Christmas: Deck the Desktop you shake the window; in Snowly the snow falls when you close or minimize it. Several build a drift along the bottom of the screen as well.

In Wonderfall this works with Snow accumulation on, in Settings > Surface and tools. Snow gathers on the top edges of your open windows and moves with them, a bank builds along the bottom of the screen, and a quick drag sends a window’s snow sliding off. It needs the Full screen weather area and the Metal renderer, and very small windows don’t collect snow. Since version 1.6.1, a snowman or a Christmas tree can stand on a window top too.

Can you shovel the snow off your Mac screen?

In most snow apps you can’t: the snow slides off when you move a window, and the drift stays or melts on its own; Snowhush, Warm Mittens and Wonderfall are the apps in the table that list a way to clear it.

Snowhush lets you sweep the drift aside with your cursor and has Clear Snow and Melt Snow in its menu. In Warm Mittens, a snowplow drives through when the drift gets deep. In Wonderfall, clearing is something you do yourself:

  1. Pick up the shovel. Choose Shovel in the menu bar menu, click it on the tool rack at the bottom of the screen, or press Control-Option-S.
  2. Dig. Click or drag across the drift to scoop the snow away. Press Esc when you’re done.
  3. Or call a snowplow. Cleanup time! sends a small vehicle along the bottom of the screen. In Surface and tools > Cleaning vehicles, pick one of four snow vehicles; with the 3D character style they have 3D artwork, and tracked ones turn their tracks as they drive.
  4. Or play with it. Choose Play in the snow, roll snowballs along the drift and stack three into a snowman, then right-click it to dress it in the Snowman Wardrobe. The snowman guide shows how.

Is there a free snow app for Mac?

Yes: Snowhush and Let It Snow Desktop are free, and Snow Now, SnowWall and Warm Mittens are free with a paid Pro upgrade.

Snowhush is free and source-available, under a license that allows noncommercial use, and you download it from its website or GitHub rather than the App Store. Snow Now says snow on your windows is always free, with extras such as the Santa train in Pro. SnowWall needs macOS 26 or later. Wonderfall has no free tier: it is a one-time US$3.99 download with no subscription.

Should you get a snow app from the Mac App Store or a website?

Both work, but they differ in how you pay and update: Mac App Store apps are bought with your Apple Account and update through the App Store, while website apps use a license key or a download you update yourself.

Apple’s guide to safely opening apps on your Mac explains that Apple reviews the apps in the App Store, and that macOS checks apps from elsewhere before they open. KoiArt emails you a license key after you buy on its site (more in our KoiArt comparison), and so does CozyMac, which we compare with Wonderfall in a separate guide; Snowhush comes as a disk image from its website or GitHub. Wonderfall is on the Mac App Store, so there is no license key to keep track of, and updates arrive through the App Store.

Which snow apps work after winter?

Several do: KoiArt, Snowhush, SnowWall and Wonderfall all list rain, leaves or blossom besides snow.

Wonderfall has rain with drops that collect on the screen and a squeegee to wipe them, autumn leaves and spring petals that pile up for the broom, summer fireflies and your own images falling. Turn on Automatically match local weather, and it picks snow or rain from the weather outside, with a seasonal effect on other days.

Why choose Wonderfall as your snow app?

Choose Wonderfall if you want snow you can do things with: it piles on your windows, you dig it out with a shovel or a snowplow, and you build and dress a snowman in it, all in one Mac App Store purchase.

  • Snow your way. Ten flake shapes, three presets (Gentle, Balanced, Intense) and Custom sliders for intensity, size, speed and wind.

Where should you start?

Get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (US$3.99 once in the US), choose Snow, turn on Snow accumulation in Surface and tools and let it snow for a while. When a bank has built up along the bottom, press Control-Option-S and dig your Dock out.
