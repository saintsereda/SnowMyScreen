Wonderfall 1.6.1 is the 3D update to the Mac desktop effects app, out on the Mac App Store since November 23, 2026. Santa and his reindeer, the penguins, snowmen, Christmas trees, the tools and the cleaning vehicles get new 3D artwork, and you can drop a tree or a snowman on top of an app window, where it rides along as the window moves.

Version 1.6 brought penguins, the Snowman Wardrobe and more Christmas trees. Version 1.6.1 changes how all of them look and where they can stand: switch between Pixel and 3D in one place, give every tree its own decorations, and let a snowman sit on your browser window. If you already have Wonderfall, it arrives as a regular update in the App Store.

How do you switch Wonderfall between Pixel and 3D?

Open Wonderfall’s Settings, go to General and set Character style to Pixel or 3D: the whole cast on your desktop changes at once.

  1. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Settings…
  2. Open General.
  3. Under Character style, pick 3D for the new artwork or Pixel for the pixel art you know from earlier versions.

The switch covers Santa and his reindeer, the penguins, snowmen (and the previews in the Snowman Wardrobe), Christmas trees, the tool cursors and the tool rack, and all 13 cleaning vehicles, including the picture tiles in Settings. Tracked vehicles now turn their tracks as they drive. New installs start with 3D; if you are updating from 1.6, Wonderfall keeps Pixel until you switch, so nothing changes on your desktop until you decide.

Can you put a Christmas tree on top of a window on your Mac?

Since Wonderfall 1.6.1 you can drag a Christmas tree and drop its base on the top edge of an ordinary app window: the tree stands there and follows the window when you move it.

  1. Put a tree on your desktop: keep Christmas tree on in Settings > Holiday, or open Decoration Inventory from the Wonderfall menu and click Place.
  2. Hover over the tree; the first time, it shows “Drag to move · Right-click to decorate”.
  3. Drag the tree up and drop its base on the top edge of a window, such as Finder or your browser.
  4. Move the window around. The tree rides along on top of it.

If the window closes, is covered or gets too small, the tree goes back down to the bottom of the screen. While it stands on a window, the tree is drawn above the window’s content, even if the weather display layer is set to Desktop only. Floating panels and menus don’t hold trees. The Christmas tree guide shows how to set up and decorate your tree.

Can a snowman stand on a window too?

A finished snowman can stand on a window too: in snow play, lift it with the mitten and drop it on the top edge of an app window, and it moves with the window.

Only the snowman’s bottom ball touches the window. If the window closes or gets covered, the snowman falls back into the snow, and two snowmen can’t share one spot. Snow play itself works as before: it needs the snow season, Snow accumulation on, the Full screen weather area and Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. The snowman guide explains how to roll the balls and build one.

Can each Christmas tree have its own decorations?

Since 1.6.1, every Christmas tree you place from Decoration Inventory keeps its own decorations, so one tree can be Classic and the next one Winter.

Right-click a placed tree to decorate it: the changes apply only to that tree. Trees you placed in 1.6 keep the look they had. The main tree still follows the choices in Settings > Holiday. Dragging feels better too: a placed decoration stays solid and follows the pointer without jumping, and if you drag it over a spot where it can’t go, it shows back where it was standing.

What else changed in 1.6.1?

New users get a short guided tour of snow play, the tools explain themselves when you hover over them, and the Snowman Wardrobe has a Santa Hat.

  • A six-step tour on a fresh install in the snow season: open the menu, “Pick up the mitten”, “Roll a snowball”, “Build a snowman”, “Throw a snowball” and “Dress your snowman”, with Skip and Next. Each step moves on by itself once you do it, and snow piling up is on from the first launch, so you can start playing right away.
  • Hover hints on the tool rack: “Shovel · clears snow”, “Mitten · roll, build, throw”, “Squeegee · wipes the glass” and “Broom · sweeps leaves”. A new snowman shows “Right-click to dress it”. To see the hints again, click Show again next to Snow play tips in Settings > Surface and tools.
  • Santa Hat joins the Hat tab of the Snowman Wardrobe, right after Beanie.
  • The wardrobe editor has left Settings: right-click a snowman to dress it, and use Make Default in the wardrobe to dress new snowmen the same way. The Snow play part of Settings keeps a Reset button, shown only while a default outfit is set.

What does 1.6.1 not change?

The falling weather and the menu bar lights stay as they were: Character style leaves the snow, rain, leaves, petals, fireflies and lights alone.

Penguins don’t stand on windows; they still run along the bottom of the screen when you choose Call Penguins. The penguin guide shows what they do. The guided tour appears only once, on a fresh install, and can’t be replayed. Santa’s frequency and sleigh sound settings work as before, and the lights still hang along the menu bar. How do you get Wonderfall 1.6.1?

If you already have Wonderfall, update it in the App Store; if not, download it from the Mac App Store, where it costs US$3.99 once in the US and needs macOS 14.6 or later.

  1. Open the App Store on your Mac and click Updates in the sidebar (Apple’s guide to updating apps also shows how to turn on automatic updates).
  2. Click Update next to Wonderfall.
  3. Open Wonderfall’s Settings… from the menu bar, go to General and set Character style to 3D.

Then drag your Christmas tree onto the top of the window you work in most, and choose Call Santa now to see the 3D sleigh fly by. Get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store and give your winter desktop its new look tonight.
