To make it snow on your Mac or MacBook desktop, install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store, click its icon in the menu bar and choose Season > Snow. Snow starts falling over your desktop right away. Set the display layer to Desktop only to keep it behind your windows or Over windows to see it while you work, and turn on Snow accumulation to let it pile up.

A still wallpaper can set the mood, but a little movement changes how a workspace feels. Wonderfall adds snow as a transparent layer, so you keep your favorite background and go on using your apps. This guide explains the setup, the controls worth changing first, snow that piles up at the bottom of the screen and on top of your windows, and the other seasons. It was updated on November 27, 2026 for Wonderfall 1.6.1, which added 3D characters and snowmen and trees that stand on your windows.

Is snow on a Mac a wallpaper, a screensaver or something else?

Wonderfall’s snow is a desktop overlay: a transparent layer of animation that stays on screen while you work, in front of or behind your windows, and lets every click pass through to your apps.

It doesn’t install a new wallpaper or replace the macOS screensaver, so your own background stays. If you are weighing a snow wallpaper or screensaver instead, the animated snow guide compares all three. How do you make it snow on your Mac or MacBook?

Install Wonderfall, choose Snow and pick where it falls; the same steps work on a MacBook, an iMac or any other Mac with macOS 14.6 or later.

  1. Check compatibility. Wonderfall supports macOS 14.6 and later, on Apple silicon and Intel. Find it on the Mac App Store.
  2. Install and open the app. Look for its icon in the menu bar. A new install starts with snow that piles up, and during the snow season it offers a six-step tour: open the menu, pick up the mitten, roll a snowball, build a snowman, throw a snowball and dress your snowman. Follow it or press Skip; it isn’t shown again.
  3. Turn on snowfall. Choose Season > Snow in the menu, or open Settings… > Weather, make sure Weather effects is on and choose Snow. There you can also pick Rain, Autumn leaves, Spring petals, Summer fireflies or Your image for your own pictures.
  4. Choose placement. Start with Desktop only for a subtle background effect. Use Over windows if you want snow visible over your open windows.
  5. Pick a preset. Gentle, Balanced and Intense set the amount and size of the flakes in one click. Choose Custom to set Intensity, Flake size, Speed and Wind yourself.
  6. Choose the flakes. Pick one of the ten built-in flake shapes or mix several. A slower fall looks gentler; Wind sets the direction, and Drifting adds sway.

If a full-screen effect is too much, choose the top strip. It confines the weather effect to the upper 150 points of the screen. This is useful when you want a hint of movement near the menu bar while leaving most of the workspace clear.

How do you keep the snow out of your way while you work?

For reading, writing or a busy desktop, try Desktop only, the Gentle preset and a slower speed; your app windows then cover the snow wherever they overlap the wallpaper. For a more visible seasonal setup, switch to Over windows and raise the intensity.

The right balance depends on your wallpaper and screen size. Bright flakes are easier to see on a dark background; pale wallpapers may need a larger flake size or a different shape. Change one control at a time so you can see what makes the difference.

If you want a quiet moment, choose Pause all effects in the menu bar menu, or turn the weather effects off, without opening Settings.

Can snow pile up at the bottom of your Mac screen?

Yes: with Snow accumulation on in Wonderfall’s Settings > Surface and tools, falling snow settles along the bottom of your Mac screen and builds a drift over time.

The faster the snow falls, the faster the drift grows, so a higher intensity fills it sooner. Turning on Snow accumulation switches the weather area to Full screen by itself, because a drift needs the whole screen. When the drift gets deep, clear it one of two ways:

  • Dig it out with the shovel. Choose Shovel in the Wonderfall menu, click the shovel on the small tool rack at the bottom of the screen, or press Control-Option-S in any app. Click or drag to dig, then press Esc to put the shovel back. The shovel makes a short sound while you dig.
  • Call a snowplow. Cleanup time! in the Wonderfall menu, also a button in Surface and tools, sends a small vehicle across the bottom of the screen to clear the snow. Choose which one under Cleaning vehicles: the classic tractor-style snowplow by default, or one of three other styles. With the 3D character style, the vehicles have 3D artwork, and tracked ones turn their tracks as they drive. The vehicles are silent.

With the Automatic corner, the tool rack sits in the bottom-right corner unless your Dock is on the right; choose another corner, or hide the rack with Show tools on screen, under Tools in Surface and tools. Hover over a tool to see what it does. The Control-Option-S shortcut can be changed in Settings > Shortcuts.

Can snow settle on top of your windows?

Yes, since Wonderfall 1.3: with Snow accumulation on, snow also gathers on the top edges of your open windows, moves with them and slides off when you drag a window quickly.

Wonderfall reads only where your windows are, never their titles or what is inside them, so it needs no Screen Recording permission. Windows smaller than 120 by 60 points are ignored. Window snow needs the Full screen weather area and Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. Autumn leaves and spring petals settle on window tops the same way, and rain splashes into droplets on window edges.

Since version 1.6.1, a finished snowman or a Christmas tree can stand there too. Lift a snowman with the mitten, or drag a tree, and drop it on the top edge of an app window: it rides along as the window moves and falls back to the bottom if the window closes or gets covered. What’s new in Wonderfall 1.6.1 shows it in action.

What else can you do with the snow on your desktop?

Besides shoveling it, you can roll snowballs and build a snowman, set up Christmas trees and Santa, call penguins, switch everything to 3D and let Wonderfall pick snow when it is snowing where you are.

  • Snowballs and a snowman. Choose Play in the snow in the Wonderfall menu or press Control-Option-P, roll balls along the drift and stack three into a snowman. Right-click a snowman to dress it in the Snowman Wardrobe: hat (including a Santa Hat), face, buttons and clothes. The snowman guide shows how.
  • Christmas trees and Santa. A small decorated tree stands at the bottom of the screen, on the drift when there is one, and Decoration Inventory in the menu places more; each placed tree keeps its own decorations. Santa and his reindeer fly across the screen every so often. The Christmas tree guide shows how to decorate the trees and set up Santa.
  • Penguins. Choose Call Penguins in the Wonderfall menu, and three penguins run in, belly-slide across the snow, wave and run off. How to call penguins onto your desktop.
  • Pixel or 3D. In Settings > General, Character style switches Santa, the penguins, snowmen, trees, tools and vehicles between Pixel and 3D. New installs start with 3D; if you updated from 1.6, you keep Pixel until you switch. The snowflakes themselves stay the same.
  • Snow when it snows outside. Turn on Automatically match local weather in Settings > Weather. Wonderfall uses Apple WeatherKit and your approximate location to show snow when it is snowing where you are, rain when it rains and a seasonal effect on other days, and it checks again every 30 minutes.

Can you try rain, leaves, petals and fireflies?

Yes: Wonderfall offers a theme for every season beyond snow, and the homepage videos show how each one looks.

  • Rain: choose a drizzle, steady rain or shower preset, then adjust the drop size, wind and intensity. This is a visual effect; it does not play rain audio.
  • Autumn leaves: amber and crimson leaves drift and tumble. Wind and drifting controls let you change their movement.
  • Spring petals: blush-colored petals give the desktop a softer seasonal look.
  • Summer fireflies: warm lights move through the scene rather than filling it with falling flakes.

You can choose each theme yourself. Optional weather matching uses Apple WeatherKit and needs internet access and location permission. Explore the desktop effect videos to compare the options.

Can your own images fall instead of snowflakes?

Yes: choose Your image, and your own pictures fall instead of snowflakes. Add pictures with the file picker or by dragging them into the gallery. Wonderfall takes up to 10 images in common formats or SVG and prepares the size automatically. A transparent background helps the artwork blend with your desktop instead of appearing as a falling rectangle.

Select one image or mix several, then choose snow-like, leaf-like or rain-like motion. Use your own art for a seasonal celebration or a personal desktop decoration, and keep it small enough that you can still read and work comfortably.

Can you add decorative lights, with or without snow?

Yes: Wonderfall’s lights hang along the menu bar with or without snow, because they have separate controls from the weather effects. Turn on Light Strip in Settings > Lights, pick one of eight scenes such as Holiday Lights or Winter Night, then change the palette, bulb shape and animation. Use lights on their own, combine them with snow for Christmas or pair a warm palette with autumn leaves. The Christmas lights guide covers the main lights settings, from scenes to your own colors.

What should you check if the snow is not visible?

Confirm that the app is running, Weather effects is enabled and effects aren’t paused. In Desktop only mode, a maximized window can cover the animation; reveal the wallpaper or try Over windows. Check the selected season and increase intensity if it is too subtle.

If the snow falls but never piles up, or the shovel, window snow and snowman are missing, check two things. First, drifts, window snow, tools and snow play need the Full screen weather area; they don’t work in the Top strip. Second, they need Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. If Metal is not available on your Mac, Wonderfall switches to a simpler renderer: snow still falls, but drifts and tools are hidden, and Settings says “Tools need the Metal renderer, which is not available on this computer.”

The app creates an overlay for each connected display and updates it when the display setup changes; Settings > General > Displays turns each display on or off. If you still need help, visit setup and support or email Andrew with your app version, macOS version and display setup.

Where should you start?

Start with one simple effect and make small adjustments: get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), choose Snow with the Gentle preset, and turn on Snow accumulation when you want it to pile up. You don’t need a new wallpaper or a complicated setup to give your desktop a different season.
