To add falling snow to your Mac desktop, download Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. Open its settings from the menu bar and choose Snow in Weather. Choose Desktop only to keep the animation behind your windows, then adjust the intensity, size, speed and wind.

A still wallpaper can set the mood, but a little movement changes how a workspace feels. Wonderfall adds seasonal animation as a transparent layer, so you can keep your favorite background and continue using your apps. This guide explains the setup, the controls worth changing first and how to move beyond snow. It was updated on November 22, 2026 for Wonderfall 1.6 and now also covers snow that piles up at the bottom of the screen and on top of your windows.

Desktop overlay, live wallpaper or screensaver?

These are different ways to decorate a Mac. A wallpaper is the background behind your windows. A screensaver appears when the Mac is idle. A desktop overlay adds a separate layer of animation that can stay visible while you work.

Wonderfall uses overlays. It does not install a new wallpaper or replace the macOS screensaver. In Desktop only mode, effects appear behind your open windows. Over windows mode places them in front. The overlay ignores mouse clicks, so it does not stop you from clicking a document, an app or a desktop icon. If you came looking for Google’s “let it snow” trick, here is what we found when we tried it in 2026 and how to get snow over your whole screen.

How to put falling snow on your Mac desktop

  1. Check compatibility. Wonderfall supports macOS 14.6 and later. Find it on the Mac App Store.
  2. Install and open the app. Look for its icon in the menu bar. Open settings from there; the effect does not need an open settings window to keep running.
  3. Turn on snowfall. In Weather, make sure Weather effects is on and choose Snow, then pick a built-in snowflake. You can also choose Rain, Autumn leaves, Spring petals, Summer fireflies or Your image for your own pictures.
  4. Choose placement. Start with Desktop only for a subtle background effect. Use Over windows if you want snow visible over your open windows.
  5. Adjust intensity and size. Start with a small amount of smaller flakes. Increase either control until the snowfall is noticeable without covering the details of your work.
  6. Set speed and wind. A slower fall gives a gentler result. Wind changes the direction; drifting adds movement to the snow. Pick a built-in flake shape you like.

If a full-screen effect is too much, choose the top strip. It confines the weather effect to the upper 150 points of the screen. This is useful when you want a hint of movement near the menu bar while leaving most of the workspace clear.

Find a setup that stays out of your way

For reading, writing or a busy desktop, try Desktop only, small particles and a slower speed. Your app windows then cover the effect wherever they overlap the wallpaper. For a more visible seasonal setup, switch to Over windows and adjust the density.

The right balance depends on your wallpaper and screen size. Bright flakes are easier to see on a dark background; pale wallpapers may need a more visible shape or a different theme. Change one control at a time so you can see what makes the difference.

If you prefer a quieter setup, reduce intensity or turn off the weather effect when you do not want it. Use the menu bar to change or stop the effect without keeping settings open.

Can snow pile up at the bottom of your Mac screen?

Yes. Turn on Snow accumulation in Wonderfall’s Settings > Surface and tools, and the falling snow settles along the bottom of the screen and builds a drift over time.

The faster the snow falls, the faster the drift grows, so a higher intensity fills it sooner. Turning on Snow accumulation switches the weather area to Full screen by itself, because a drift needs the whole screen. When the drift gets deep, clear it one of two ways:

  • Dig it out with the shovel. Choose Shovel in the Wonderfall menu, click the shovel on the small tool rack at the bottom of the screen, or press Control-Option-S in any app. Click or drag to dig, then press Esc to put the shovel back. The shovel makes a short sound while you dig.
  • Call a snowplow. Cleanup time! in the Wonderfall menu, also a button in Surface and tools, sends a small vehicle across the bottom of the screen to clear the snow. Choose which one under Cleaning vehicles: the classic tractor-style snowplow by default, or one of three other styles. The vehicles are silent.

With the Automatic corner, the tool rack sits in the bottom-right corner unless your Dock is on the right; choose another corner, or hide the rack with Show tools on screen, under Tools in Surface and tools. The Control-Option-S shortcut can be changed in Settings > Shortcuts.

Can snow settle on top of your windows?

Yes, since Wonderfall 1.3: with Snow accumulation on, snow also gathers on the top edges of your open windows, moves with them and slides off when you drag a window quickly.

Wonderfall reads only where your windows are, never their titles or what is inside them, so it needs no Screen Recording permission. Windows smaller than 120 by 60 points are ignored. Window snow needs the Full screen weather area and Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. Autumn leaves and spring petals settle on window tops the same way, and rain splashes into droplets on window edges. The Wonderfall 1.3 release post introduced it.

What else can you do with the snow on your desktop?

Besides shoveling it, you can roll snowballs and build a snowman, set up a Christmas tree and Santa, call penguins, and let Wonderfall switch to snow when it is snowing where you are.

  • Snowballs and a snowman. Choose Play in the snow in the Wonderfall menu or press Control-Option-P, roll balls along the drift and stack three into a snowman. Since 1.6, click a snowman to open the Snowman Wardrobe and change its hat, face, buttons and clothes. The snowman guide shows how.
  • A Christmas tree and Santa. A small decorated tree stands at the bottom of the screen, on the drift when there is one, and Santa and his reindeer fly across the screen every so often; both are in Settings > Holiday. The Christmas tree guide shows how to decorate the tree and set up Santa.
  • Penguins. New in 1.6: choose Call Penguins in the Wonderfall menu, and three penguins run in, belly-slide across the snow, wave and run off. How to call penguins onto your desktop.
  • Snow when it snows outside. Turn on Automatically match local weather in Settings > Weather. Wonderfall uses Apple WeatherKit and your approximate location to show snow when it is snowing where you are, rain when it rains and a seasonal effect on other days, and it checks again every 30 minutes.

Try rain, leaves, petals and fireflies

Wonderfall offers seasonal themes beyond snow. The homepage videos show how each one looks:

  • Rain: choose a drizzle, steady rain or shower preset, then adjust the drop size, wind and intensity. This is a visual effect; it does not play rain audio.
  • Autumn leaves: amber and crimson leaves drift and tumble. Wind and drifting controls let you change their movement.
  • Spring petals: blush-colored petals give the desktop a softer seasonal look.
  • Summer fireflies: warm lights move through the scene rather than filling it with falling flakes.

You can choose each decorative theme yourself. Optional weather matching uses Apple WeatherKit and needs internet access and location permission. Explore the desktop effect videos to compare the options.

Animate your own images

Choose Your image and add pictures with the file picker or by dragging them into the gallery. Wonderfall takes up to 10 images in common formats or SVG and prepares the size automatically. A transparent background helps the artwork blend with your desktop instead of appearing as a falling rectangle.

Select one image or mix several, then choose snow-like, leaf-like or rain-like motion. Use your own art for a seasonal celebration or a personal desktop decoration, and keep it small enough that you can still read and work comfortably.

Add decorative lights, with or without weather

Garlands have separate controls from Weather effects. Decorate the top menu bar. Choose a color palette, bulb shape and steady, twinkle, wave or chase animation. Use lights on their own, combine them with snow for Christmas or pair a warm palette with autumn leaves. The Christmas lights guide covers the main lights settings, from scenes to your own colors.

What should you check if the snow is not visible?

Confirm that the app is running and Weather effects is enabled. In Desktop only mode, a maximized window can cover the animation; reveal the wallpaper or try Over windows. Check the selected theme and increase intensity if it is too subtle.

If the snow falls but never piles up, or the shovel, window snow and snowman are missing, check two things. First, drifts, window snow, tools and snow play need the Full screen weather area; they don’t work in the Top strip. Second, they need Wonderfall’s Metal renderer. If Metal is not available on your Mac, Wonderfall switches to a simpler renderer: snow still falls, but drifts and tools are hidden, and Settings says “Tools need the Metal renderer, which is not available on this computer.”

The app creates an overlay for each connected display and updates it when the display setup changes. If you still need help, visit setup and support or email Andrew with your app version, macOS version and display setup.

Start with one simple effect and make small adjustments: get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), choose Snow with the Gentle preset, and turn on Snow accumulation when you want it to pile up. You do not need a new wallpaper or a complicated setup to give your desktop a different season.
