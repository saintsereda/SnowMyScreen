What is CozyMac?

CozyMac is a Mac app that draws weather on a transparent layer over your desktop, lets snow pile up on your windows, folders and Dock, and comes with ten 4K wallpapers.

Its website lists ten weather scenes: snow, rain, storm, leaves, petals, fireflies, meteors, mist, cloud and clear, and the weather can follow the forecast for your city. From the menu bar you can call Santa, who drops a present, bury the desktop in snow, freeze the screen, light a fireplace that crackles and melts the snow, decorate a tree or start a Christmas light show on your windows that syncs to music. Holding Option while you drag through the snow rolls a snowman; a snowplough digs out a buried desktop, and a tiny snowcat clears the Dock.

Is there a free version of CozyMac?

CozyMac has no free version, as far as its website shows: on November 28, 2026, cozymac.app listed a single price, US$9.99 once for one Mac, and mentioned no free version, trial or demo.

The site adds that there is no subscription and no account. Wonderfall isn’t free either, but it costs less: US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store, with no subscription and no in-app purchases. And because it is an App Store purchase, you can request a refund from Apple the usual way if it isn’t what you wanted.

Do you need an activation key for CozyMac?

CozyMac needs an activation key: you pay for it through a Stripe link on its website, the key arrives by email, and you activate the app by opening its Settings → License and pasting the key.

The app itself comes from the same website as a disk image that you drag to Applications, and the site says it is notarized by Apple. Keep the email with your key: we found nothing on the site about recovering a lost key or moving it to another Mac. Wonderfall has no key at all. You buy it with your Apple Account, there is nothing to paste or keep, and updates arrive through the App Store like any other app.

Is there a CozyMac for Windows?

CozyMac’s website doesn’t mention a Windows version: on November 28, 2026, cozymac.app described a Mac app for macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon and Intel, with no Windows version.

Wonderfall is a Mac app too, for macOS 14.6 or later on Apple silicon and Intel Macs, and there is no Windows version of it either.

Why get a snow app from the Mac App Store?

An App Store app leaves you nothing to keep track of: you buy it with your Apple Account, it has no license key, and it updates through the App Store together with your other apps.

Apple’s guide to safely opening apps on your Mac explains that Apple reviews the apps in the App Store, and that macOS checks apps from elsewhere before they open; CozyMac’s site says it is notarized by Apple. Wonderfall also needs no account and no sign-in, and snow on your windows reads only where your windows are, never what’s in them, so it asks for no Screen Recording permission.

What does Wonderfall do with snow?

In Wonderfall, snow is something you can work with: it piles up on your windows and along the bottom of the screen, and you shovel it, plow it or build a snowman out of it.

  • Snow that piles up. Turn on Snow accumulation in Settings > Surface and tools (on a new install of version 1.6.1 it is on from the first launch). Snow gathers on the top edges of your open windows and moves with them, a drift builds along the bottom of the screen, and a quick drag sends a window’s snow sliding off.
  • A shovel in your hand. Pick it up from the tool rack at the bottom of the screen or press Control-Option-S, click or drag to dig, and press Esc to put it back.
  • Snowplows. Cleanup time! sends a small vehicle along the bottom of the screen. Choose one of four snow vehicles in Cleaning vehicles.
  • Snowballs and snowmen. Choose Play in the snow or press Control-Option-P, roll balls along the drift, stack three into a snowman and throw snowballs. Right-click a snowman to dress it in a hat (Santa Hat included), a face, buttons and a scarf, or lift it with the mitten onto the top of a window.
  • Snow your way. Ten flake shapes, the Gentle, Balanced and Intense presets, and Custom sliders for intensity, flake size, speed and wind. Keep it Over windows while you work or Desktop only behind your apps.

What else does Wonderfall add for Christmas?

Wonderfall adds lights along the menu bar, Christmas trees you place and decorate one by one, Santa flying past and penguins sliding through, and since version 1.6.1 all the characters can be drawn in 3D.

  • Lights. In Settings > Lights, turn on Light Strip and choose Holiday Lights or one of seven other scenes. With Switch light styles for holidays on, the string changes to the Velvet Holiday palette with gift-shaped bulbs from December 1 to 26, then switches back. The lights guide covers every setting.
  • Christmas trees. Decoration Inventory in the Wonderfall menu places more trees, and each one keeps its own style, topper and ornaments; right-click a tree to decorate it. Drop a tree on the top edge of a window, and it rides along when you move the window. The Christmas tree guide shows how.
  • Santa. Set Santa flyover in Settings > Holiday to Sometimes (roughly every 10 to 25 minutes) or Often (roughly every 3 to 8), or choose Call Santa now. The sleigh sound can be turned off.
  • Penguins. Choose Call Penguins, and three penguins run in, belly-slide across the snow, wave and run off.
  • Pixel or 3D. Character style in Settings > General switches Santa, the penguins, snowmen, trees, tools and vehicles between pixel art and 3D at once. Here is everything new in 1.6.1.

What does CozyMac have that Wonderfall doesn’t?

CozyMac has wallpapers and sound that Wonderfall doesn’t: it comes with ten 4K wallpapers, a crackling fireplace and a light show that syncs to music, and it lists scenes such as storm, meteors and mist.

Wonderfall doesn’t install or replace wallpapers; it decorates the wallpaper you already have. It has no ambient sound: only the hand tools, snow play and Santa’s sleigh make a sound. Its lights hang along the menu bar, not on your windows, and it isn’t a screensaver. What you get instead is snow you can shovel, plow and build with, a holiday scene you can arrange yourself, and rain, leaves, petals and fireflies for the rest of the year, all in one App Store purchase.

How do you switch from CozyMac to Wonderfall?

Quit CozyMac so you don’t get two layers of snow, install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store and turn on the snow and the lights; it takes a few minutes.

  1. Quit CozyMac from its menu bar icon.
  2. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store.
  3. Choose snow. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar, choose Settings…, open Weather and pick Snow with the Gentle or Balanced preset.
  4. Let it pile up. In Surface and tools, make sure Snow accumulation is on.
  5. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick Holiday Lights.
  6. Follow the weather, if you like. Automatically match local weather in Weather picks snow or rain from the weather outside, using Apple WeatherKit and your approximate location.

Turn on Launch at login in General, and it all comes back after a restart.
