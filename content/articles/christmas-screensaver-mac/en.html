The simplest Christmas screensaver on a Mac is made from your own holiday photos: in System Settings, open Wallpaper, then Screen Saver, choose Photos and point it at a folder of Christmas pictures. macOS has no Christmas-themed screen saver of its own, and any screen saver runs only while your Mac sits idle. For snow, lights and Santa while you work, use a desktop overlay such as Wonderfall.

Search for a Christmas screensaver for Mac and most of what comes up was written for Mac OS X: the top results still include an Engadget roundup from 2009 and another from 2005. This guide covers what works on a current Mac: the built-in screen saver with your own photos, what to check before you install an old download, and how to keep the screen festive while you are actually using it.

Does macOS have a Christmas screensaver?

No. Apple’s guide lists the built-in screen savers as macOS, aerials of landscapes, cityscapes, underwater scenes and Earth, Mac, Shuffle Aerials and Other, which includes your own photos, and none of them is Christmas-themed.

So a Christmas screensaver on a Mac comes from one of two places: your own photos, shown by the built-in Photos screen saver, or a screen saver file downloaded from the web. The list above comes from Apple’s Mac User Guide for macOS 27, checked on November 19, 2026. A MacBook uses the same settings as any other Mac.

How do you make a Christmas screensaver from your own photos?

Put your Christmas photos in one folder and choose that folder in the built-in Photos screen saver.

  1. Collect the photos. Save the holiday pictures you want to see into one folder, for example “Christmas”.
  2. Open the settings. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, then click Wallpaper in the sidebar.
  3. Go to the screen saver. Click Screen Saver, then Custom.
  4. Pick Photos. Scroll to Other, click Photos, then click Options.
  5. Choose your folder. In the pop-up menu, choose Choose Folder, select your Christmas folder and click Choose. Photo Library is there too, but it shows your whole library.
  6. Pick a style. Click Style to choose how the photos move, then click OK.

These steps follow Apple’s guide to using your photos as a screen saver for macOS 27. On an older macOS the names and places can differ a little; Apple’s guide has a version menu at the top.

How do you choose when the screensaver starts?

macOS starts the screen saver once your Mac has been idle for a while; the idle settings are in System Settings > Lock Screen.

To start it right away, set up a hot corner: in System Settings > Desktop & Dock, click Hot Corners, choose the screen saver option for one corner and click Done. Moving the pointer into that corner then starts your Christmas photos (Apple’s guide to hot corners). Press a key, move the mouse or touch the trackpad to stop it.

Is it safe to download a Christmas screensaver for Mac?

Only from a source you trust: a downloaded screen saver is software that runs on your Mac, and many Christmas screen savers still listed online were made for Mac OS X more than a decade ago.

macOS checks downloaded software with Gatekeeper. If it blocks a file from an unidentified developer, you can still open it with Open Anyway in System Settings > Privacy & Security, but Apple warns that overriding security settings to open an app is the most common way a Mac gets infected with malware (Apple’s guide to opening apps from unidentified developers). Since macOS Sequoia, the old Control-click shortcut no longer skips this check, as MacRumors reported. If you don’t know who made a file, leave it.

How do you keep a festive screen while you work?

Use a desktop overlay instead of a screen saver: an overlay draws snow, lights and other decorations on a transparent layer over your desktop while you use your Mac, and your clicks pass straight through it.

Wonderfall is that kind of app, not a screen saver. It doesn’t use or replace the macOS screen saver and doesn’t change your wallpaper, so your photo screen saver can stay exactly as you set it up. How do you set up a Christmas desktop with Wonderfall?

Turn on Snow, the menu bar lights and the Christmas tree in Wonderfall’s Settings, and the scene stays on screen while you work.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later.
  2. Open Settings. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Settings…
  3. Add snow. In Weather, choose Snow and a preset: Gentle, Balanced or Intense.
  4. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick the Holiday Lights scene. The string hangs along the menu bar.
  5. Add the tree. In Holiday, keep Christmas tree on and choose Small, Medium or Large. Since version 1.5 you can also give it a topper and ornaments.
  6. Choose where it shows. In Weather and in Lights, the Display layer setting offers Over windows, in front of your apps, or Desktop only, behind them.

For the lights in detail, from scenes to your own colors and an optional Christmas style that switches on by itself on December 1, read how to put Christmas lights on your Mac screen.

Can Santa fly across your Mac screen?

Yes. Wonderfall’s Santa flyover sends Santa and his reindeer across your screen every so often while you work.

In Settings > Holiday, set Frequency to Sometimes, roughly every 10 to 25 minutes, or Often, roughly every 3 to 8 minutes. The sleigh sound can be turned off, and Call Santa now sends him right away. Santa doesn’t fly while Reduce motion is on or when a full-screen app covers the display.

Common questions

Is Wonderfall a screensaver?

No. It is a desktop overlay that runs while you work. It doesn’t use or replace the macOS screen saver, so you can keep a Christmas photo screen saver as well.

Can I use Christmas photos from my Photos library?

Yes. The Photos screen saver can show your Photo Library. To show only Christmas pictures, save them to a folder and choose that folder instead.

Does a Christmas screensaver work on a MacBook?

Yes. A MacBook has the same screen saver settings as other Macs, so the steps above work on it too.

Where should you start?

Use both: a photo screen saver for the moments your Mac sits idle, and an overlay for the hours you work at it.
