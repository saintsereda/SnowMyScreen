What is KoiArt?

KoiArt is an app by Spacerr LLC that puts animated weather and Christmas decorations on your screen and comes with painted wallpapers; it runs on Mac and on Windows.

Its home page lists six weather effects (snow, leaves, starfall, rain, sakura and fireflies), Christmas decorations such as up to three trees, lights, snowmen, Santa riding by, a yeti and a train with an elf delivering gifts, a Christmas screen saver, and nine painted wallpapers with a day and night option. On a Mac it needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Do you need a license key for KoiArt?

KoiArt does use a license key: you buy it on its website, the key arrives by email after you pay, and you paste it into the app; to move to another computer, its site says to remove the key on the old one first.

Wonderfall has no license key. You buy it in the Mac App Store with your Apple Account, there is nothing to paste or keep safe, updates arrive through the App Store like any other app, and there is no account to create.

How much does KoiArt cost compared with Wonderfall?

KoiArt costs US$9 once for one computer, with a separate license for up to five computers, Mac or PC; Wonderfall costs US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store.

KoiArt’s site says there is no free plan, but a short tour shows every effect on your screen before you pay. Neither app has a subscription. Wonderfall has no in-app purchases either: the one purchase includes every season, tool and decoration in the app. Prices were checked on November 28, 2026, and can change during the holidays.

Does KoiArt run on Windows, and does Wonderfall?

KoiArt has a Windows version: its site lists Windows 10 and Windows 11 next to macOS. Wonderfall runs only on Macs, with macOS 14.6 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.

If snow on a Mac is what you need, that is exactly what Wonderfall is built for: it lives in the menu bar, decorates the wallpaper you already have and draws the snow with Metal, the Mac’s graphics framework.

KoiArt and Wonderfall both put weather and Christmas decorations on your screen; the biggest differences are where you buy them, how you unlock them and which computers they run on.

What does Wonderfall give you?

Wonderfall gives you a winter scene you can play with: snow that piles up and that you clear with tools in your hand, Christmas trees that can stand on top of your windows, a string of lights along the menu bar, and Santa, snowmen and penguins in 3D.

  • Pixel or 3D. Since version 1.6.1, Character style in Settings > General switches Santa and his reindeer, the penguins, snowmen, Christmas trees, the tools and the cleaning vehicles between pixel art and 3D at once; new installs start with 3D. Here is everything new in 1.6.1.
  • Lights along the menu bar. Turn on Light Strip in Settings > Lights and pick one of eight scenes, such as Holiday Lights or Winter Night, then change the palette, bulb shape, animation, brightness and glow. Turn on Switch light styles for holidays, and the string turns Christmas-colored from December 1 to 26 by itself. The Christmas lights guide covers every setting.
  • Your own images. Choose Your image, add up to ten pictures, and they fall like snow, tumble like leaves or drop like rain.
  • Your local weather. Automatically match local weather uses Apple WeatherKit and your approximate location to pick snow or rain from the weather outside, with a seasonal effect on other days.

How does Wonderfall’s snow work?

Wonderfall’s snow falls, piles up and can be cleaned up or played with: with Snow accumulation on in Settings > Surface and tools, a drift builds along the bottom of the screen and on the top edges of your open windows.

A quick drag sends a window’s snow sliding off. The tool for the current weather stands on a small rack at the bottom of the screen: a shovel for snow, a squeegee for raindrops on the screen, a broom for leaves and petals. Pick it up with a click or Control-Option-S, use it, and press Esc to put it back, or choose Cleanup time! to send one of four snow vehicles instead. Choose Play in the snow or press Control-Option-P to roll snowballs and stack a snowman, then right-click it to dress it, Santa Hat included. Snow on windows reads only where your windows are, never what’s in them, so Wonderfall needs no Screen Recording permission. The snow guide explains every control.

Can Wonderfall put Christmas trees, Santa and penguins on your desktop?

Wonderfall can do all three: it places extra Christmas trees from Decoration Inventory, each decorated on its own, sends Santa and his reindeer across the screen and calls penguins to slide through the snow.

Right-click a tree to choose its Tree style (Classic, Winter, Cozy or Minimal), Tree topper, Ornaments and Ornament colors; since version 1.6.1 every tree placed from Decoration Inventory keeps its own decorations. Drag a tree onto the top edge of an app window, and it stands there and follows the window when you move it. Set Santa flyover in Settings > Holiday to Sometimes, roughly every 10 to 25 minutes, or Often, roughly every 3 to 8, or click Call Santa now. Choose Call Penguins, and three penguins run in, belly-slide across the snow, wave and run off. The Christmas tree guide covers it step by step.

What does Wonderfall leave out?

Wonderfall doesn’t come with wallpapers, isn’t a screensaver and runs only on Macs, and its lights hang only along the menu bar.

It decorates the wallpaper you already have instead of replacing it, and it has no ambient sound: only the hand tools, snow play and Santa’s sleigh make a sound, and the sleigh can be turned off. What it does have is a complete winter and holiday scene in one Mac App Store purchase, plus rain, leaves, petals and fireflies for the rest of the year.

How do you switch from KoiArt to Wonderfall?

Switching from KoiArt takes a few minutes: quit it so you don’t get two layers of snow, install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store and turn on snow, lights and a tree in its Settings window.

  1. Quit KoiArt.
  2. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  3. Turn on snow. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar, choose Settings…, open Weather, and choose Snow with the Gentle or Balanced preset.
  4. Let it pile up. In Surface and tools, make sure Snow accumulation is on.
  5. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick Holiday Lights.
  6. Add a tree. Choose Decoration Inventory in the Wonderfall menu and click Place next to the Christmas tree.

Turn on Launch at login in General to have it all back after a restart. If you came here looking for KoiArt, get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store for a one-time US$3.99, turn on the snow and the Holiday Lights, and pick up the shovel when the first drift builds.
