Snowy! and Let It Snow are both snow apps in the Mac App Store. Snowy! is free and, since November 14, 2026, can let snow gather on your windows; Let It Snow costs US$4.99, was last updated on September 26, 2026, and lists only a drift along the bottom of the screen, in its Pro upgrade. Wonderfall is the alternative where snow piles up on your windows and you shovel it, plow it or build a snowman, for US$3.99 once.

This guide is about the Mac apps. If you typed “let it snow” into Google hoping for snow on your screen, that is a different thing, and here is what we found when we tried it. Below we look at what Snowy! and Let It Snow do, using their App Store listings checked on November 28, 2026, and compare them with Wonderfall. What is Snowy! for Mac?

Snowy! is a free menu bar app by LVL35 LLC that lets snow fall in front of or behind your windows and hangs string lights across the top of the screen; it needs macOS 15.6 or later.

According to its App Store listing, snow ranges from a light flurry to a full blizzard, the lights come in five color themes with twinkle, wave or steady animation, and the app can switch your wallpaper to one of its bundled holiday wallpapers. Version 6.11.11, released on November 14, 2026, added optional accumulation on window tops, the Dock and the bottom of the screen (off by default), which you clear by moving windows or sweeping the pointer through the drift, plus an option to run the lights around the screen. The listing shows no in-app purchases.

Is Let It Snow for Mac still updated?

Yes: Let It Snow by Gavin Matthews, first released in 2011, got version 2026.2 on September 26, 2026, which its release notes call “A big winter update!”

That update, per the App Store listing, added a breeze and a Wind slider, depth so that nearer flakes look larger and faster, and Pause Snow in the menu bar, and the snow now pauses while the Mac sleeps or is locked. The app costs US$4.99 and needs macOS 14.6 or later. The free part has snowfall you can set by amount, size, speed, flurries and wind, with an option to snow behind the active window. A US$2.99 in-app purchase, Let It Snow More, adds flake styles, colored snow, your own image as a snowflake, a permanent drift at the bottom of the screen, snow on up to four displays, and festive lights and a hat on the active window. The listing doesn’t mention snow settling on windows.

Is Let It Snow the same as Let It Snow Desktop?

No: Let It Snow Desktop is a separate, free app by a different developer, Vladyslav Ilyenko, whose snow stays behind your windows and can follow the live weather.

Its version 2.0 came out on November 8, 2026, according to its App Store listing. Neither app is Google’s “let it snow” search trick, which was an effect on the search page, not something you install.

Which Mac snow app lets snow pile up on your windows?

Wonderfall lets snow pile up on your windows, and so does Snowy! since its November 14 update; Let It Snow’s listing describes a drift along the bottom of the screen in Pro, but no snow on windows.

In Wonderfall, turn on Snow accumulation in Settings > Surface and tools; on a new install of version 1.6.1 it is on from the first launch. Snow gathers on the top edges of your open windows and moves with them, a drift builds along the bottom of the screen, and a quick drag sends a window’s snow sliding off. The faster it snows, the faster the drift grows. It needs the Full screen weather area and the Metal renderer, and very small windows don’t collect snow. Wonderfall reads only where your windows are, never what’s in them, so it asks for no Screen Recording permission.

How do you clear the snow in Wonderfall?

Wonderfall lets you clear snow by hand or with a snowplow: pick up the shovel and dig, or choose Cleanup time! and a small vehicle clears the bottom of the screen for you.

  1. Pick up the shovel. Click it on the tool rack at the bottom of the screen, choose Shovel in the menu bar menu or press Control-Option-S.
  2. Dig. Click or drag across the drift to scoop the snow away, and press Esc when you’re done.
  3. Or call a snowplow. Cleanup time! in the menu sends a vehicle along the bottom of the screen. Pick one of four in Settings > Surface and tools > Cleaning vehicles; with the 3D character style they have 3D artwork, and tracked ones turn their tracks as they drive.

Can you build a snowman on your Mac?

In Wonderfall you can: choose Play in the snow or press Control-Option-P, roll balls along the drift, stack three into a snowman and throw snowballs at it.

Right-click a finished snowman to dress it in the Snowman Wardrobe: hats such as a beanie, a top hat or a Santa Hat, faces, buttons and scarves, with Make Default for every new snowman. Since version 1.6.1 you can also lift a snowman with the mitten and set it on the top edge of a window, where it rides along. The snowman guide goes through it step by step.

What does Wonderfall add beyond the snow?

Wonderfall adds a whole holiday scene and the rest of the year: lights along the menu bar, Christmas trees, Santa and penguins, plus rain, leaves, petals and fireflies when winter is over.

  • Lights. Turn on Light Strip in Settings > Lights and pick Holiday Lights or another of eight scenes; Switch light styles for holidays makes the string Christmas-colored from December 1 to 26. The lights guide covers the rest.
  • Trees, Santa and penguins. Place Christmas trees from Decoration Inventory and decorate each one, call Santa’s sleigh across the screen and send three penguins sliding through the snow, in Pixel or 3D (Character style in Settings > General).
  • Other seasons. Rain with drops on the screen and a squeegee, autumn leaves and spring petals that pile up for the broom, summer fireflies, and up to ten of your own images falling like snow. Automatically match local weather picks snow or rain from the weather outside.
  • Every display. Effects appear on each connected display, and you can turn each one on or off.

What do Snowy! and Let It Snow have that Wonderfall doesn’t?

Snowy! is free, comes with holiday wallpapers and can run its lights around the screen; Let It Snow’s Pro upgrade puts lights and a festive hat on the active window.

Wonderfall costs US$3.99 once and has no free tier. It doesn’t install or replace wallpapers; it decorates the one you already have. Its lights hang along the menu bar only, not around the screen edge or on windows, and it isn’t a screensaver. What it gives you instead is snow you can shovel, plow and build with, and a holiday scene you arrange yourself, all in one App Store purchase with no subscription.

Where should you start?

Start by installing Wonderfall from the Mac App Store, choosing Snow and letting it pile up; before long you’ll have a drift to dig into.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. If Snowy! or Let It Snow is running, quit it so you don’t get two layers of snow.
  2. Choose snow. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar, choose Settings…, open Weather and pick Snow with the Gentle, Balanced or Intense preset.
  3. Let it pile up. In Surface and tools, make sure Snow accumulation is on.
  4. Hang the lights. In Lights, turn on Light Strip and pick Holiday Lights.

If you came here looking for Snowy! or Let It Snow because you want snow that does more than fall, get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store for a one-time US$3.99, let it snow for a while, and press Control-Option-S when the first drift has built up along the bottom.
