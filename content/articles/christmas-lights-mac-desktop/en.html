To put Christmas lights on your Mac screen, install Wonderfall, open Settings > Lights and turn on Light Strip: animated bulbs appear along the menu bar. Pick the Holiday Lights scene, and turn on Switch light styles for holidays to have the bulbs change to gifts in the Velvet Holiday palette by themselves from December 1 to 26. The string hangs along the menu bar, not around the whole screen.

Lights are the smallest holiday decoration a computer can have: they stay at the top of the screen, out of the way of your work, and make the whole desktop feel warmer. This guide was updated on November 27, 2026 for Wonderfall 1.6.1, the current version: the lights can sway in the wind, glow in your own colors and switch to a Christmas style by themselves on December 1, and Christmas trees with matching lights can now stand on top of your windows.

How do you put Christmas lights on your Mac screen?

You turn on Light Strip in Wonderfall’s Lights settings, and the string appears along the menu bar right away.

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
  2. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Settings…
  3. Open Lights in the sidebar and turn on Light Strip. Bulbs appear along the menu bar at the top of the screen.
  4. Pick a scene under Scenes to set the palette, bulb shape and animation in one click, then fine-tune anything you like.

After that you don’t need Settings to switch the lights on or off: the Wonderfall menu in the menu bar has a Festive lights toggle.

Can the lights go around the border of the screen?

No. Wonderfall hangs its lights along the menu bar at the top of the screen; they don’t run down the sides, along the bottom, on the Dock or around your windows.

Some other Mac apps do frame the screen. On November 27, 2026, the App Store listings of Desktop Lights and A Coolbeans Christmas said they string lights around the edges of the screen, and Festive Lights – Mood Lights offers the Dock, the menu bar or the screen edges; Festivitas, sold on its own website, decorates the Dock and the menu bar. The Christmas apps comparison shows what each one does. In Wonderfall, you can make the top edge look richer and decorate the bottom edge with other winter effects:

  • Make the string denser. In Lights, click Customize next to Shape. Lower Spacing (24 to 72 pt, 36 by default) so the bulbs sit closer together, and raise Bulb size (8 to 20 pt, 12 by default).
  • Change how the wire hangs. The same panel has Segment width, Wire sag and Hanging length, added in version 1.5.
  • Turn up the glow. Click Customize next to Light & motion and raise Glow and Brightness for a bigger halo around each bulb.
  • Frost the top edge. Turn on Snow and set Weather area to Top strip: the snow then falls only in the top 150 points of the screen, where the lights hang.
  • Or decorate the bottom edge. Keep the weather area on Full screen, turn on Snow accumulation in Surface and tools so a drift builds along the bottom, and let the Christmas tree stand on it. The drift needs Full screen, so choose either this or the Top strip.

Which light scene should you choose for Christmas?

For Christmas, start with Holiday Lights or Winter Night: a scene sets the palette, bulb shape and animation in one click, and you can change any of them afterwards.

  • Holiday Lights: the classic pick for December.
  • Winter Night: a winter scene that still fits in January and February.
  • Cozy Evening: for dark afternoons and evenings at your desk.
  • New Year and Celebration: for the last week of December and any party. The bulb picker even has digit shapes that spell 2027.
  • Halloween, Spooky Night and Spring Bloom: for the rest of the year, so the string can stay up after the holidays.

How do you change the colors, bulbs and glow?

Every part of a scene can be changed in Lights: the color palette, the bulb shape, the animation pattern, brightness and glow.

  • Color palette: 12 palettes, with Festive colors as the default. Color order runs the colors in order, in soft groups or mixed.
  • Bulb shape: 20 shapes, from the classic oval to gifts, pine trees, stars, hearts and cats.
  • Pattern: 10 animations, including Steady, Twinkle (the default), Wave, Chase, Candlelight, Sparkle and Slow wave. Speed and Motion set how fast and how much they move.
  • Brightness and Glow: how strong the bulbs are and how big the halo around them is.

Since version 1.5 the lights can also glow in your own colors. Click Customize next to Color palette, turn on Use custom colors and pick one to six colors in the standard macOS Colors window. It starts from the palette you had. Choosing a scene turns custom colors off, so pick the scene first and the colors after.

If Reduce motion is on in System Settings > Accessibility > Motion, the lights stay steady instead of blinking.

Can the lights sway in the wind?

Yes, since Wonderfall 1.5: with Sway with weather on, the string swings in the wind of the weather falling on your screen, in the same direction and with the same strength.

The switch is in Lights under Light & motion, and it is off until you turn it on. Wind sensitivity sets how much the bulbs move, from 0 to 100% (65% by default). The lights sway only while a weather effect such as snow is running and Reduce motion is off. The wind comes from the app’s own snow, rain, leaves or petals, not from the weather outside, so for a gentle swing give the snow a light Wind in its Custom settings.

Can the lights switch to a Christmas style by themselves?

Yes. Turn on Switch light styles for holidays, and from December 1 to 26 the lights use the Velvet Holiday palette with gift-shaped bulbs. On December 27 they switch to the New Year style, and after January 1 they go back to your own settings.

You’ll find it in Lights under Holiday Calendar; it is off until you turn it on. Each holiday can be turned on or off separately, and the dates follow your Mac’s local time, first and last day included. The two winter holidays, as set since Wonderfall 1.5:

Only the palette and bulb shape change. Animation, brightness, bulb size and spacing stay as you set them, and your own palette and shape come back when the holiday is over. If you change the scene, palette, shape or custom colors during a holiday, the holiday style pauses until that holiday ends. Valentine’s Day and Halloween have styles too; the Wonderfall 1.5 release post lists them all. For the last week of December, the New Year desktop guide covers the 2027 bulbs and the rest of the New Year scene.

Do the lights work with the Christmas tree?

Yes. The Christmas tree on your desktop has its own lights that follow the palette, animation, brightness and speed of the menu bar string, so one change in Lights recolors both.

There are exceptions: custom colors, holiday styles and sway apply to the menu bar string only, not to the tree. Turn the tree on or off in Settings > Holiday (Christmas tree) or in the menu bar menu, choose Small, Medium or Large, and drag it sideways along the bottom of the screen; it keeps clear of the Dock. You can give it a topper, ornaments and ornament colors, and Decoration Inventory places more trees, each with its own decorations.

Since version 1.6.1 you can also drop a tree on the top edge of an app window, where it rides along as the window moves, and its lights keep following the palette, animation, brightness and speed of the string. Character style in Settings > General switches the trees between Pixel and 3D, but it doesn’t change the lights themselves. What’s new in Wonderfall 1.6.1 shows trees on windows.

Santa is a separate setting in the same Holiday section and doesn’t change the lights, but he completes the scene: Santa flyover sends Santa and his reindeer across the screen every so often. Sometimes means roughly every 10 to 25 minutes, Often roughly every 3 to 8. Call Santa now sends him right away. Santa doesn’t fly while Reduce motion is on or when a full-screen app covers the display.

Do the lights stay visible over maximized windows?

Yes, when the lights’ Display layer is set to Over windows: they stay in front of your open windows, including one you have maximized, and clicks pass through to whatever is underneath.

With Desktop only, the lights stay behind your open windows, so a maximized window hides them. The lights have their own Display layer, separate from the weather, so they can stay on top while the snow stays behind your work. Based on the app’s settings since version 1.5:

Switch between them in Lights under Display layer, or from the Lights display submenu in the Wonderfall menu. With Hide lights on hover on, which is the default, the bulbs fade when the pointer passes over them, so you can see what is beneath.

How do you add snow under the lights?

Turn on Snow in Wonderfall’s Weather settings: lights and weather are independent, so you can use either one on its own or both together.

For a full winter scene, keep the lights along the top and let the snow fall below them. Here is how snow on your desktop works, and how it differs from a wallpaper or a screensaver. For ready-made combinations of wallpaper, snow and lights, see how to give your Mac a Christmas theme.

Common questions

Do the lights replace my wallpaper?

No. They are drawn on a transparent layer along the top of the screen. Your wallpaper and windows stay as they are.

Can I put the lights on the Dock?

No. The string hangs along the menu bar only, and there is no setting to move it to the Dock.

Is there a Christmas lights wallpaper for my MacBook?

Not from Apple: its wallpaper guide for macOS 27 lists dynamic wallpapers, aerials, colors and your own photos, with no holiday wallpaper (checked November 27, 2026). Wonderfall doesn’t install wallpapers either. It hangs moving lights over whichever wallpaper you choose, so your own Christmas photo can glow under a real string of bulbs.

Can I have lights without snow?

Yes. Lights and weather effects have separate controls.

Are the lights only for Christmas?

No. There are scenes for winter, New Year, Halloween, spring and any celebration, and the holiday styles also cover Valentine’s Day and Halloween, so the string can stay up all year.

Where should you start?

Start with four steps: get Wonderfall on the Mac App Store (a one-time US$3.99 in the US), turn on Light Strip with the Holiday Lights scene, switch on Switch light styles for holidays so the string turns Christmas on December 1, and add a gentle snowfall underneath.
