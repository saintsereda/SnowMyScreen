Does Google’s “let it snow” trick still work?

Not in our test: on November 20, 2026, a search for “let it snow” on google.com showed no snow at all.

We ran the search in a Chromium-based browser on a Mac, once with English (United States) results and once with Ukrainian (Ukraine) results. Both pages showed music videos and a panel about the song “Let It Snow! Let It Snow! Let It Snow!”, which Sammy Cahn and Jule Styne wrote in 1945. Nothing fell, the page did not frost over, and there was no Defrost button.

Other sources disagree with each other. Search Engine Magazine, in a guide updated June 18, 2026, says you can’t see the effect on Google anymore. Quick Online Tips, in a list of Google Easter eggs updated October 21, 2026, still describes it and says Google shows it only in winter. Neither page says when the search was last tried.

Why is “let it snow” not working on Google?

There is no official answer: we found no statement from Google about retiring the “let it snow” Easter egg, and Search Engine Magazine says there isn’t one either.

So be careful with posts that give a firm reason or a removal date; we found none that quotes Google. Our own test can’t explain it either: it only shows that no snow appeared in a current browser on that day. And if the effect does come back in December, as the winter-only explanation suggests, it will still fall only inside the Google tab. Switch to your email or a document, and the snow is out of sight.

What did Google’s “let it snow” Easter egg do?

It turned the results page into a frosty window: snowflakes fell over the search results, the page slowly clouded over, and the search button became a Defrost button that cleared it.

Google added it in December 2011, as TechCrunch reported on December 17, 2011. Two days later, Search Engine Roundtable noted that the snow piled up until the results were hard to read, and that you could wipe it away with the mouse or click Defrost. It was back in December 2013, when IBTimes Australia described the frost and the Defrost button again.

Can you still see the “let it snow” effect anywhere?

Yes, in a fan recreation, but only inside its own browser tab.

It is on elgoog.im, an independent project that states it is not affiliated with Google. According to its page, the copy looks like a search results page: snow falls, frost spreads, you can draw through the frost with the mouse, and Defrost starts it over. Like the original, it lives in one tab, and the rest of your screen stays as it was.

How do you make it snow on your whole Mac screen?

Use a desktop overlay: an app that draws snow on a transparent layer in front of all your windows, while your clicks pass straight through to the apps underneath.

Wonderfall is that kind of app for the Mac. Its snow falls over every app, not only the browser, settles on the top edges of your open windows and piles up along the bottom of the screen. You set it up once:

  1. Install Wonderfall from the Mac App Store. It needs macOS 14.6 or later.
  2. Open Settings. Click the Wonderfall icon in the menu bar and choose Settings…
  3. Choose Snow. In Weather, pick Snow and the Gentle preset for a calm snowfall. Balanced and Intense are the stronger presets.
  4. Cover the whole screen. In the same section, set Weather area to Full screen and Display layer to Over windows. Snow now falls in front of your apps, and your clicks still go through.
  5. Let it settle. In Surface and tools, turn on Snow accumulation. Snow gathers on top of your windows and moves with them, and a drift builds along the bottom of the screen.

Drag a window quickly, and its snow slides off. When the drift gets deep, pick up the Shovel from the menu bar menu or press Control-Option-S, click or drag to dig, and press Esc when you’re done. Cleanup time! sends a small snowplow across the screen instead. The main snow settings are covered in how to add snow to your Mac desktop.

What should you know before you install Wonderfall?

Wonderfall is a Mac app, not a browser trick, so a few things work differently from Google’s Easter egg.

  • Mac only. There is no Windows, iPhone or iPad version.
  • A one-time purchase. It costs US$3.99 once in the US Mac App Store. Prices in other regions may differ.
  • Window snow needs the full screen. Snow on windows, the drift and the shovel need the Full screen weather area and the Metal renderer. If Metal is unavailable on a Mac, snow still falls, but drifts and tools are hidden. Very small windows don’t collect snow.
  • Your wallpaper stays. Wonderfall is neither a screensaver nor a wallpaper app: it draws on a transparent layer and leaves your desktop picture as it is.

Common questions

Will the snow get in the way of my clicks?

No. Wonderfall’s layers ignore mouse clicks, so your apps, documents and desktop icons work as usual.

Can I keep the snow behind my windows?

Yes. Set Display layer to Desktop only, and the snow falls only on the desktop, behind your apps, almost like part of your wallpaper. A maximized window hides it.

Does it work with more than one display?

Yes. Wonderfall shows effects on every connected display, and Settings > General > Displays lets you turn each one on or off.

Can I build a snowman in the drift?

Yes. Choose Play in the snow in the menu bar menu, roll snowballs along the drift and stack three into a snowman. The snowman guide shows how.
